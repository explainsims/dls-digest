# Monday, 25 May 2026

## Top story

**The Gemini story has moved from launch theatre to implementation discipline. Quietly, thank God.**

The weekend did not bring a new education bombshell. The useful signal is that school-facing Gemini adoption is now being written about less as "look what AI can do" and more as "what training, support, and expectations make it usable." A 22 May EdTech Magazine piece on K-12 Gemini adoption is vendor-adjacent, but the case examples are still useful: districts are using Gemini for planning, scheduling comparisons, meeting summaries, staff communication, Drive/Docs/Sheets workflows, and student-data/budget analysis, while emphasizing purpose, training, champions, and consistent expectations. ([EdTech Magazine](https://edtechmagazine.com/k12/article/2026/05/districts-rely-training-and-implementation-support-google-gemini-adoption))

That matters for international schools because Google AI is no longer a separate "AI tool" conversation. It is settling into the Workspace layer teachers and leaders already use. The practical school question is therefore not whether Gemini exists. It is whether a school can name the approved use cases, the data boundaries, the staff training path, and the admin controls before the tool becomes informal infrastructure by habit.

Google's own education push points the same way. Its AI Educator Series, announced with ISTE+ASCD, is framed as free AI literacy training for U.S. K-12 and higher-ed educators, with modules released monthly. Its wider education update also ties Gemini, NotebookLM, Moodle LTI, NotebookLM limits, and student test-prep into one package. ([Google for Education](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/ai-tools-programs-educators/))

For Ed Tech Coaches and school technology leaders, the lesson is usefully dull: start with routines, not marvels. Email drafting, meeting summaries, source-bound research, curriculum planning, and data-table interpretation are the places staff will actually encounter Gemini. That is where norms need to live. If the first staff PD asks "what can Gemini do?", it will drift into party tricks. If it asks "which school workflows may Gemini touch, under what conditions, and how do we know?", it has a chance of becoming governance.

- **NotebookLM inside Workspace Studio is still the highest-risk useful Google update on the table.** Google's 12 May update makes existing notebooks usable as an AI knowledge source in Workspace Studio flows through an "Ask NotebookLM" step. It is available by default if Gemini for Google Workspace steps are allowed, including Education Fundamentals, Standard, Plus, Google AI Pro for Education, and Teaching and Learning add-ons. ([Google Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/05/notebooklm-in-workspace-studio.html)) This is not new since Friday, but it remains the operational hinge: a school notebook can become a workflow source. That turns source quality, ownership, and review dates into live governance, not filing etiquette.

- **Google's AI control center is the admin-side story to pair with every Gemini enthusiasm session.** Google describes the control center as a centralized Admin console view for generative AI and agent access to Workspace data, including visibility into AI usage across Gmail, Drive, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Meet, Calendar, Chat, and the Gemini app. It also surfaces security controls, trust rules, classification labels, and data-protection rules. ([Google Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/05/securely-manage-AI-and-agent-access-to-Workspace-data-with-the-AI-control-center.html)) Schools should ask what can be seen, what can be disabled, and what still falls outside the dashboard's polite little fence.

- **OpenAI's Edu admin surface is converging on the same governance shape.** The latest ChatGPT Enterprise/Edu release notes now include admin-console areas for Analytics and Agents, with visibility into active users, message activity, GPTs, projects, skills, tool interactions, connector interactions, and workspace agents. The same notes flag ChatGPT for Excel and Google Sheets for Enterprise, Edu, and K-12 workspaces, with workspace controls including RBAC, residency where available, EKM, and Compliance API coverage. ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10128477-chatgpt-enterprise-edu-release-notes)) Even for Google-first schools, this matters as a market signal: vendors know schools will ask for control planes. Procurement should make them prove those controls work in the school's actual data shape.

- **AILit is moving toward classroom exemplars, not just framework language.** The AI Literacy Framework team says the final version is due in the coming weeks and will be accompanied by two ready-to-use multilingual exemplar activities: one primary activity around categorization and one secondary activity around verifying LLM outputs. ([AILit Framework](https://ailiteracyframework.org/blog/bringing-ai-literacy-to-life-exemplars-for-classroom-use/)) This is useful because AI literacy work needs examples teachers can adapt inside subjects. Frameworks are necessary. So are activities with desks, timings, misunderstandings, and students in the room.

## Sceptic's corner

The claim to challenge today is that "training" solves AI adoption.

Training helps. Bad training is still worse than no training because it gives everyone the confidence to be casually wrong in the same direction. A two-hour Gemini workshop can teach useful habits: source checking, prompt discipline, privacy boundaries, and where not to use the tool. It can also accidentally teach staff that every problem is now a Gemini-shaped problem, which is how schools end up with automated meeting notes, AI-polished family communications, and spreadsheet summaries before anyone has decided what data is allowed to move.

The real test is whether training changes defaults. After a session, do staff know which student data can go into Gemini? Do leaders know which workflows need review? Do departments know when NotebookLM is personal research and when it becomes a school knowledge base? Can IT see enough to help, or only enough to be blamed later?

AI PD should not be a confidence bath. It should create shared limits, shared vocabulary, and a small set of approved routines. Everything else is just product onboarding wearing a lanyard.
