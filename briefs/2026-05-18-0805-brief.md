# Monday, 18 May 2026

## Top Story

**Canvas breach keeps turning into the LMS governance story.**

Instructure says it reached an agreement with the hackers behind the Canvas breach to have stolen data deleted, after disruption hit students during finals and attackers claimed access involving roughly 8,800-9,000 institutions and about 275 million people. The reported exposed categories include names, email addresses, student IDs, and platform communications; Instructure says passwords, government IDs, dates of birth, and financial data were not involved. ([AP](https://apnews.com/article/canvas-outage-college-students-exams-grades-3d55b9399ae87d49276f354e1c34c180), [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/education/2026/05/09/canvas-school-hack-trend/), [EdTech Magazine](https://edtechmagazine.com/k12/article/2026/05/canvas-breach-what-k-12-leaders-need-know-about-third-party-saas-risk), [Education Week](https://www.edweek.org/technology/a-cyberattack-on-canvas-could-cause-lasting-aftershocks-for-schools/2026/05)).

The lesson for international schools is broader than one vendor. Learning platforms have become identity, communication, assessment, and record hubs. Any LMS review should ask what data lives in the platform, which integrations touch it, how SSO is scoped, whether sensitive communications are stored there, and what the vendor's breach process actually requires.

**Hype vs substance:** The headline numbers are still being clarified. The operational lesson is already clear: the longer-term risk after an education-platform breach is often phishing, impersonation, and social engineering, not only the immediate outage.

## Other Stories

### 1. Google I/O starts tomorrow, and Gemini will be everywhere

Google's official I/O note says the May 19-20 event will focus on AI breakthroughs across Gemini, Android, and more. For schools, the useful watch is not the consumer demo layer; it is Workspace and Education availability, admin controls, age-gating, and how Gemini features interact with Drive, NotebookLM, AI Studio, and student accounts. ([Google I/O](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/io-2026-save-the-date/)).

### 2. Google's AI Educator Series launched last week

Google's education update says the ISTE+ASCD-partnered AI Educator Series was due to begin 13 May, with modules released monthly. The materials may be useful for professional learning, but they should be treated as vendor-authored training rather than neutral pedagogy. ([Google education update](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/ai-tools-programs-educators/)).

### 3. OpenAI is explaining privacy while facing a tracking lawsuit

OpenAI published a plain-language guide on how ChatGPT training and privacy choices work. Separately, a California lawsuit alleges ChatGPT.com disclosed user query-related information to Meta and Google through tracking code. The allegations are unproven, but the timing reinforces a school-facing point: privacy claims need contractual and technical verification, not only public explanations. ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/how-chatgpt-protects-privacy/), [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/415131/openai-sued-for-allegedly-disclosing-queries-to-me.html)).

### 4. Microsoft's education AI controls remain a useful comparison case

Microsoft's education guidance says "Teach" in the Microsoft 365 Copilot app is available for faculty/staff SKUs, enabled by default when Copilot Chat is enabled, and not available to students. "Learning Activities" are available to students 13+ but require admin deployment steps. The useful comparison is the shape of the controls: age thresholds, defaults, role separation, and admin disable paths. ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/education/guide/1-reference/manage-microsoft-365-education-ai-features), [Microsoft Education](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/education/ai-in-education)).

### 5. Apple's AI story is still privacy-first, but mostly "watch WWDC"

Apple-focused reporting over the weekend points to WWDC 2026 as the next checkpoint for Apple Intelligence, with privacy and on-device/local processing still central to the story. For schools using Apple devices, the practical question is how much of the AI experience can be governed locally and how much still depends on cloud services and account-level controls. ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/05/17/privacy-data-security-will-remain-tantamount-for-apples-2026-ai-push)).

## Sceptic's Corner

The strongest sceptic argument this week is about infrastructure, not nostalgia. Students generally do not choose the platforms schools require them to use, so the privacy burden sits with institutions. Every new account, integration, and AI feature should earn its place through a clear learning purpose, a defensible data model, and an exit plan.
