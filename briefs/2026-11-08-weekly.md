## What Does It All Mean?

Two of the week's stories sit on opposite sides of one screen. Guided Learning now gives teachers "individual student chat transcripts" ([Google, 4 November](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/11/educators-can-now-add-guided-learning-to-assignments-in-Google-Classroom-to-help-students-get-extra-support.html)). A lawsuit alleges that a 13-year-old discussed suicidal thoughts with Google's AI on a school Chromebook for months before her school alerted her parents, and that Google does not provide chat data on student accounts ([Bloomberg via Claims Journal, 5 November](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/11/05/341139.htm)). So the Sunday question: should a school read its students' AI conversations, and if it does, what is it reading?

Start with what a transcript is evidence of. Nicholas Soderstrom and Robert Bjork's review of the motor- and verbal-learning research separates performance, what a learner can do during practice, from learning, the change that lasts. The first is often an unreliable index of the second, and learners often mistake one for the other ([Soderstrom and Bjork, 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25910388/)).

AI supplies a vivid demonstration. In a field experiment with nearly 1,000 Turkish high-school maths students, those given an unguarded GPT-4 tutor did 48% better than the control group in practice, then 17% worse on the exam once it was taken away. A hint-giving version lifted practice by 127% and left exam scores about level ([Wharton, August 2024](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education/); since published in [PNAS, 2025](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122)). A smooth transcript records performance with help on hand. It shows where a student got stuck. It cannot show what they will know next month.

Then consider what being read does. Help-seeking is social, and part of AI's appeal is that no one is watching. When researchers surveyed 6,960 students at four Australian universities in 2024, 49% were using AI for feedback, and one wrote that "[AI] allows me to ask stupid questions that I'm too ashamed to ask my teacher" ([The Conversation, 27 August 2025](https://theconversation.com/uni-students-are-using-ai-to-ask-stupid-questions-and-get-feedback-on-their-work-263535)). Put the teacher inside the chat and some of that safety leaves.

School monitoring shows where reading drifts. In the Center for Democracy and Technology's research, approximately half of students said they were not comfortable expressing their true thoughts and feelings online when they knew they were monitored. Among teachers at schools using monitoring software, 78% reported students flagged for disciplinary action, against 54% reporting referrals to a counsellor or other adult ([CDT, August 2022](https://cdt.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Hidden-Harms-The-Misleading-Promise-of-Monitoring-Students-Online-Research-Summary-Final-Accessible.pdf)). Software bought for care slides towards discipline.

The lawsuit shows the opposite failure: a private channel with no adult anywhere near it. So the answer is not to look away. It is to look narrowly, and to say so out loud.

For a school, that means three rules before any course switches on Guided Learning insights. Students hear, at the start of the task and in plain words, who can read the chat. Transcripts are read to find misconceptions, never marked or used for discipline. Safeguarding runs through the trained route the school already has, not a class teacher's dashboard. An AI use scale tells students what they may use. It does not yet tell them who is reading.

## Behind You! Behind You!

**A family is suing Google over a school Chromebook.** Parents of a 13-year-old North Carolina seventh-grader have sued in California state court, alleging she spent months discussing suicidal thoughts with Gemini and Search's AI Mode, and that the warning came from her school, not Google ([Bloomberg via Claims Journal, 5 November](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/11/05/341139.htm)). Google told the New York Times it is reviewing the claims ([SheKnows via Yahoo, 6 November](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/parents-sue-google-over-teen-173408311.html)). These are untested allegations. For any Google school, the question is which of its own alerts would fire on a chat.

**Deepfakes are outrunning the rulebook.** One in three of 852 US educators surveyed by the EdWeek Research Center this summer don't know whether their school has a policy on AI-generated fake images and videos; nine in 10 haven't been trained to detect or respond to deepfakes in the past two years ([EdWeek via GovTech, 5 November](https://www.govtech.com/education/k-12/deepfake-cyber-bullying-outpaces-teacher-training-policies)). The same week Google paused a Vids feature that built an avatar from an uploaded image, a day after announcing it. It had no admin control, listed Education Plus, and said nothing about consent from the person pictured ([Google, 2 November, updated 3 November](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/11/create-a-custom-avatar-google-vids.html)).

**Gemini now reads Forms responses.** Gemini in Google Forms will analyse responses, find patterns across questions and suggest next steps, writing and running Python code so the counts come from the actual data ([Google, 5 November](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/11/get-actionable-insights-with-gemini-in-Google-Forms.html)). It comes with Education Plus and the Google AI Pro for Education add-on, reaching Scheduled Release domains from 19 November. Course evaluations and wellbeing check-ins are now one click from a model; the setting the post names is smart features and personalisation.

## Sceptic's Corner

Google launched Guided Learning in Classroom alongside a stack of evidence: a blog promising "1.5x higher learning gains in just a single study session" ([Google, 3 November](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/guided-learning-uk-japan/)), and a teacher guide calling the tool "proven to improve learning outcomes" ([Google, Classroom guide](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/classroom_guidedlearning_guide.pdf)). Follow the numbers home.

The 1.5x compares gains of 8.1 and 5.3 percentage points after half a day of studying statistics at the University of Tokyo. Google's November AI in Education report gives the same trial as a +0.14 standard deviation improvement ([Google, November 2026](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/ai_in_education_report_nov26.pdf)). One framing sounds like half as much again; the other is a modest nudge from one sitting.

"Proven" rests on a pre-registered trial with 1,763 students in 12 schools in Port Loko, Sierra Leone, which DeepMind put at "roughly 1.2 to 1.7 years of typical learning progress" ([Google DeepMind, 9 June](https://deepmind.google/blog/measuring-the-impact-of-learning-with-ai-in-sierra-leone-and-beyond/)). The November report rounds that to between one and two years; the guide quotes 1.7, a conversion calibrated to progress in low- and middle-income countries. What was tested was a package of teacher training, paired shared devices, structured lesson plans and technical support, in a design not built to separate the tool from the rest ([EdTech Innovation Hub, June 2026](https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/google-gemini-guided-learning-raises-math-scores-in-sierra-leone-classroom-trial)). Students who started stronger gained most.

The same week, the Hechinger Report described an October study by Maryland's Jing Liu, who randomly assigned an always-available AI helper to 15 of 30 professors' courses. Students in those courses earned grades 0.11 points lower on a four-point scale, and only 15% used it ([Hechinger Report, 2 November](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-ai-tutor-results/)). Google's own report concedes that optional "tutor-on-the-side" AI in its ASU Study Hall and Coursera pilots failed to yield meaningful gains, largely through low adoption ([Google, November 2026](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/ai_in_education_report_nov26.pdf)).

The honest reading: teacher-directed AI inside a designed lesson can help. A tab students may open is a different product, and the brochure sells the second on the strength of the first.

The line for the staffroom: "proven" means it worked somewhere, for someone, as part of something. Ask which part you are buying.
