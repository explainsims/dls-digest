# 5 May 2026

## Top story — Harvard FAS drops ChatGPT Edu for Claude, and the single-vendor era of campus AI is quietly over

**Harvard's Faculty of Arts and Sciences confirmed last week it is phasing out ChatGPT Edu after June 2026 and adding Anthropic's Claude Code to its supported toolkit.** Gemini remains the primary FAS service for students; Claude is added alongside; ChatGPT becomes opt-in with budgetary approval. Reasons cited: low student uptake and cost. Anthropic the same week announced full-campus Claude agreements with Northeastern, LSE, and Champlain, plus Canvas LTI integration, MCP servers for Wiley and Panopto, and a "Learning mode" that scaffolds reasoning instead of answering. ([Harvard Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2026/4/28/fas-anthropic-claude/), [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/advancing-claude-for-education))

**Why it matters for international schools:** the framing has shifted from *"which vendor do we standardise on"* to *"which portfolio do we offer and how do we evaluate them."* Locking in a single AI provider for a curriculum-deep integration is exactly the wrong move now, when Harvard-tier institutions are actively walking back single-vendor commitments.

For Google-school stacks, Gemini stays the practical default — but a portfolio-friendly stance is the right Ed Tech Coaches posture: Claude available where it's better (long-context document work, coding, scaffolded reasoning), Gemini where workflows live in Workspace, and an explicit policy that no single vendor gets a curriculum-deep integration without a review cycle.

## Sceptic's corner

**The "Claude beats ChatGPT" narrative is louder than the data.**

A wave of April commentary — including some respectable analysis — frames Harvard's decision as part of a broader Claude-overtaking-ChatGPT story, citing revenue figures and a Pentagon contract dispute. Take this with appropriate salt. Harvard's actual reasons in the Crimson reporting are mundane: low student uptake of ChatGPT Edu specifically, and cost. Gemini remains the FAS default. That's a procurement-and-portfolio story, not a vendor-supremacy story.

The implication for international schools is the boring-but-correct one: vendors will keep leapfrogging each other through 2026-27. The question is not "who wins" but "what's the process for swapping the underperformer out without disrupting teaching." **Build for swap, not for loyalty.**
