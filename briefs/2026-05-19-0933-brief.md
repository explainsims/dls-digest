# Tuesday, 19 May 2026

## Top Story

**Google I/O day starts with the school-relevant action already in Workspace.**

Google I/O opens today, but the school-facing story is not only the keynote. The most practical signal is that Google has already been knitting Gemini and NotebookLM into the systems schools actually live in: Workspace, LMS integrations, Meet, Chrome, and admin controls.

The clearest new example is Google's 12 May expansion of **Google Workspace Assignments LTI and Gemini LTI to Moodle**, joining existing support for Canvas and Schoology. Gemini LTI brings the Gemini app and NotebookLM into third-party LMS environments, with access controlled through both Workspace admin settings and LMS admin setup. Google says students can use the integration for course-specific guidance, NotebookLM audio overviews, and AI training resources; Assignments LTI continues to handle distribution, grading, and originality reports. Availability is broad across Education Fundamentals, Standard, and Plus. ([Google Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/05/workspace-assignments-lti-and-gemini-lti-for-moodle.html?m=1)).

For international schools, this is the bit worth underlining before the I/O fireworks: Google is making Gemini portable into non-Google LMS contexts. The practical question is less "Do we use Google Classroom?" and more "Can our learning platform expose Google AI safely, governably, and without fragmenting student workflows?" That makes Gemini LTI, NotebookLM permissions, Workspace Intelligence controls, and age-based access rules central to the AI governance conversation.

I/O's Google keynote is scheduled for **19 May, 10:00 PT**. Official source: [Google I/O schedule](https://io.google/2026/?hl=en); Google's own preview says I/O will cover AI breakthroughs and updates "from Gemini to Android and more" ([The Keyword](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/io-2026-save-the-date/)).

## Other Stories

- **NotebookLM is becoming a Workspace automation source, not just a study tool.** Google added an **Ask NotebookLM** step to Google Workspace Studio flows. That means existing notebooks can serve as grounded knowledge sources inside automations, provided Gemini for Workspace steps are allowed. Rollout began 12 May for Rapid and Scheduled Release domains, with availability across Business, Enterprise, and Education editions, including Education Fundamentals, Standard, Plus, Google AI Pro for Education, and Teaching and Learning. The school interpretation: NotebookLM is moving from "student/teacher research assistant" toward "institutional workflow substrate." That is powerful, but it raises governance questions fast: whose notebook, which sources, which organizational unit, what compliance coverage, and who sees outputs? ([Google Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/05/notebooklm-in-workspace-studio.html)).

- **Workspace Intelligence gives Gemini broader context, with admin controls that matter.** Google's Workspace Intelligence update grounds Gemini tasks across Gmail, Chat, Calendar, Drive, Docs, Sheets, and Slides. Admins can disable specific data sources at the domain, organizational-unit, or group level, and Google says responses respect user-level permissions and customer data is not used for model training or ads. For schools, this is a governance story more than a productivity story. The default is "on," so the real admin work is not whether Gemini can search across Workspace; it is deciding where broad context is appropriate, where it is not, and how that maps to student age bands, staff roles, shared drives, and sensitive records. ([Google Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/04/introducing-workspace-intelligence-with-admin-controls.html?m=1)).

- **Google Meet adds explicit-consent controls for AI notes, transcripts, and recordings.** Google is rolling out an admin setting requiring participants to actively consent before automatic note-taking, recording, or transcription begins. The setting is off by default and can be enabled by domain, organizational unit, or group. Education Plus gets consent controls for transcripts and recordings; Google AI Pro for Education includes notes, recordings, and transcripts. This is immediately relevant to staff meetings, parent meetings, student support conversations, and any AI note-taking norms. The feature is not glamorous. It is exactly the kind of control that makes AI adoption survivable in schools. ([Google Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/04/require-explicit-consent-for-take-notes-with-Gemini-recordings-and-transcripts-in-Google-Meet.html)).

- **Canvas breach shifts from operational disruption to accountability.** The Canvas/Instructure story has not produced a fresh technical turn today, but the aftermath is still material. AP reports Instructure struck a deal with the hackers to return data and received "digital confirmation" of deletion, while also acknowledging there is no complete certainty when dealing with cybercriminals. TechRadar reports Instructure CEO Steve Daly has been called to testify before the U.S. House Homeland Security Committee, with the hearing expected to cover breach details, data volume, containment, and customer notification. For schools, the lesson is unchanged but sharper: vendor trust needs evidence. "Data returned" and "shred logs" are not the same as assurance. ([AP](https://apnews.com/article/canvas-outage-college-students-exams-grades-3d55b9399ae87d49276f354e1c34c180), [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/security/us-congress-calls-instructure-ceo-as-it-investigates-canvas-breach)).

- **Android's Gemini Intelligence is a student-device story in disguise.** Google's pre-I/O Android announcement pushed Gemini Intelligence toward proactive, cross-app assistance: app automation, Gemini in Chrome, smarter autofill, spoken-message rewriting, and generated widgets. Rollout starts with newer Samsung Galaxy and Pixel devices this summer, with broader device categories later. Even in Apple-forward schools, this is a student reality headline. Students will increasingly arrive with phones that can summarize, automate, fill forms, rewrite speech, and act across apps. AI policy that only thinks in terms of "websites students visit" will miss the device-layer assistant. ([Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/platforms/android/gemini-intelligence/)).

## Sceptic's Corner

Google's education AI story is getting more coherent, but also more entangling. The sales pitch is "Gemini everywhere." The operational reality is "Gemini everywhere you failed to configure carefully."

The work is now less about finding an AI tool and more about drawing boundaries: age, organizational unit, staff vs. student, Classroom vs. non-Classroom LMS, Drive scope, NotebookLM compliance coverage, Meet consent, and auditability. I/O may add shiny capabilities. The useful question afterwards is whether the admin surface kept pace.
