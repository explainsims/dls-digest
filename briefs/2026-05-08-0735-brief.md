# Friday, 8 May 2026

## Top story

**Instructure was breached again on 7 May. Canvas went dark across major institutions, school login pages were defaced with the ransom message, and the deadline shifted to 12 May — what Wednesday's coverage called a procurement signal is now a procurement event.**

Earlier coverage framed today as the leak deadline. It isn't, any more — but only because ShinyHunters escalated rather than leaked. On Thursday afternoon US-time, Canvas began intermittently failing across higher-ed; UC Irvine students saw a pop-up at 13:05 local; UC Berkeley, Princeton, MIT, Penn, Duke, Harvard, Baylor, Rutgers and a long list of K-12 districts (Charlotte-Mecklenburg, Wake County, the NC schools group) followed. Several customer login pages were defaced with injected HTML — TechCrunch confirmed three; the actual count is higher in the school-press coverage. The defacement message names the new deadline: *"You have till the end of the day by 12 May 2026 before everything is leaked."* Instructure took Canvas infrastructure offline for "scheduled maintenance," contained the intrusion, revoked privileged credentials, and as of filing time has not commented on the extortion. ShinyHunters' framing this round is sharper than last week's: *"Instead of contacting us to resolve it they ignored us and did some 'security patches'."* ([DataBreaches.net](https://databreaches.net/2026/05/07/developing-shinyhunters-hacks-instructure-again-canvas-down/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/07/hackers-deface-school-login-pages-after-claiming-another-instructure-hack/), [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/quick-takes/2026/05/07/hackers-target-canvas-again), [WRAL](https://www.wral.com/news/education/canvas-shinyhunters-ransom-instructure-hack-data-breach-may-2026/), [Daily Pennsylvanian](https://www.thedp.com/article/2026/05/penn-canvas-shinythunters-data-breach-hack-second), [UC Irvine New University](https://newuniversity.org/2026/05/07/uci-canvas-down-due-to-nationwide-hack/), [The Tech (MIT)](https://thetech.com/2026/05/07/canvas-breach-26), [Baylor ITS](https://its.web.baylor.edu/news/story/2026/update-instructure-has-taken-canvas-offline), [Daily Princetonian](https://www.dailyprincetonian.com/article/2026/05/princeton-news-stlife-canvas-hacking-outage-technology-oit-instructure-finals-period))

The structural read is clean. **Instructure has now been breached three times in eight months** — the disclosed October 2025 incident, the 3 May ransom claim, and the 7 May re-entry/defacement. The third one happened *after* the public crisis-response patches, which is the worst possible signal a vendor can send mid-contract-cycle. The boundary between "an unfortunate breach" and "a vendor-risk profile" closed yesterday afternoon. Higher-ed analyst chatter on Inside Higher Ed and Times Higher Ed is converging on personalised-phishing risk as the second-order story: with names, school emails, student IDs and *teacher-student message contents*, attackers can reproduce voice and context well enough to ride the next attack into single-sign-on harvesting. ([Times Higher Education](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/personalised-phishing-attacks-likely-after-global-canvas-hack))

The procurement signal for international schools is straightforward. Wednesday's framing was *"vendor breach posture as legitimate rubric input."* Today's is *"three breaches in eight months, with the third coming after public remediation."* If LMS evaluation rubrics still treat security as a checkbox ("vendor maintains an InfoSec program: yes/no"), this is the week to ask whether that's adequate against a 12 May leak likely to include teacher-student messages from peer institutions. The 12 May deadline falls on a Tuesday.

## Sceptic's corner

**Two corrections to earlier framing — and one to the wider narrative.**

First correction to earlier coverage: Wednesday's incident was called Instructure's *second* breach in eight months. With yesterday's re-entry, it's the *third*, and the third happened after the patches went in. That changes the rhetorical weight of the procurement argument from "this vendor has a problem" to "this vendor's remediation cycle did not work" — a different and harder thing for Instructure to recover from in the next earnings cycle.

Second: ShinyHunters' "275 million users" claim still hasn't been independently verified, and the 8,809-institution victim list has had publicly-disputed entries (some named institutions told local press they had no Canvas tenancy at all). Treat scale claims as ceiling, not floor; the practical risk to any individual student is determined by what the leak sample actually contains, which is still unknown.

Third — to the wider narrative: BETT week always produces a vendor-arms-race read. Google's free Gemini 3 Pro for educators, Microsoft's Teach module, Anthropic's M365 deep-integration and Toddle's AI Tutor Awards all landed within ten days of each other. The temptation is to read this as "the AI-in-LMS war is on" and rush into a stack decision. The honest read is the inverse: every major vendor now has *roughly* the same surface-area assistant baked into their own suite. The decision schools are actually making in 2026-27 is **whose surface area sits closest to the gradebook**, and that's a procurement question, not an AI-feature question. The Canvas breach week happens to be highlighting exactly that — what's at stake when the AI-inside-LMS gets the wrong vendor.
