## Top story

**South Korea's education minister resigned over the rollout of anti-cheating rules.** Choi Kyo-jin stepped down on 22 November after President Lee Jae Myung formally approved his resignation. Choi took responsibility for changes made just before the 19 November College Scholastic Ability Test, known as the Suneung. In a statement issued by the education ministry, he acknowledged the disruption to candidates and the criticism that his ministry should have prepared earlier. The president directed Prime Minister Han Seong-sook to oversee the follow-up. [Yonhap, 22 November](https://en.yna.co.kr/view/AEN20261122001052315).

The ministry's 20 November update counted 111 electronic-device-related cases through the final session, updating its provisional count through the first four sessions. [Education ministry tally, 20 November](https://www.moe.go.kr/boardCnts/viewRenew.do?boardID=294&boardSeq=107732&lev=0&m=020402&page=1&s=moe). The next set of exams was already bearing down. On 20 November the education ministry and the Korean Council for University Education reviewed device-cheating precautions with 46 universities that run their own admissions assessments. They set up an emergency group to share prevention and detection information until that admissions cycle's university-run exams end. On Sunday Lee said concerns had spread to university essay tests and internal school exams. He called for a cross-government review of how exams are run, including national qualifications, with changes to the law where needed. Those are directions for follow-up, with implementation still to come. [Education ministry, 20 November](https://www.moe.go.kr/boardCnts/viewRenew.do?boardID=294&boardSeq=107745&lev=0&m=020402&s=moe) · [South Korean presidency, 22 November](https://www.president.go.kr/social-media/96DfUYVL).

The immediate lesson for international schools is how a rule gets from a briefing to the exam room. An instruction about what candidates may carry is easy to circulate. Its consequences get more complicated when a student has an approved device accommodation, a substitute invigilator is on duty, or a family has read yesterday's instructions. A workable policy needs a single dated version, a route for accommodation decisions and a way for a proctor to get an answer in time to use it. Sunday's resignation is a reminder that the quality of those arrangements can become part of the fairness of the assessment itself.

## Other stories

- **Students revised with three AI tools and scored lower on a new unaided essay.** Azzeddine Boudouaia, Yan Li and Jie Gao followed 72 Zhejiang University postgraduates in an eight-week English-writing course. Students drafted three assignments independently, then revised them using ChatGPT, Pigai and Bearly, consulting their teacher when the tools' advice conflicted. Many revisions were coded as successful. On a fresh essay without AI or word-processing tools, the group's mean score for sentence-level and other rule-based features fell from 4.46 to 4.03 out of five (paired Cohen's d = 0.54). The study had one group, and the two essay prompts were not equated for rhetorical demand. Differences between the prompts or other features of the course could explain the change, and the study did not score qualities such as argument and organisation on those tests. The design does give a language department two separate things to look at. Learners may be good at applying advice to one paragraph and still need practice recognising the same problem in their next piece. Both deserve space in a writing unit. [Humanities and Social Sciences Communications, 21 November](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-026-09677-5_reference.pdf).

- **Gemini can now work inside Mac apps on eligible personal accounts.** Google announced a rollout in English in the United States for adults with personal Google AI Plus, Pro or Ultra accounts and Apple silicon running macOS 15 or later. Gemini can click, type and navigate menus, with a live preview and a stop control. People can give one-off or standing permission to an app, and Gemini asks for confirmation on sensitive tasks. Its documentation also says that access to an app can expose the accounts already signed in there. This is a consumer rollout. The school question arises where a staff member uses both personal and work services on the same device. A person may open a personal AI account and still be signed into staff mail in a native app. Treating permission as a question about the app's name alone leaves out what is open in it and what it can do. An international school managing Apple devices has a good reason to check how staff and personal sessions are separated before treating these tools as ordinary writing assistants. [Google, 19 November](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/computer-use-mac/) · [Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/18956635?hl=en).

- **The University of California widened AI access and drew a faculty governance challenge.** On 19 November UC announced a systemwide expansion of its OpenAI deal, with ChatGPT and Codex available to more students, faculty and staff at no charge to individuals. The president cited access, cost and stronger protections than people get through personal accounts. His response says campuses manage deployment locally and faculty retain authority over use in courses. The next day the Academic Council said it had asked for more consultation before the purchase was finalised and learned of the final decision about an hour before the public announcement. It called for consultation before campus deployments. The council letter and the president's reply are published together, which lets readers see when each side thought consultation could still change something. For Ed Tech Coaches, that is worth asking before a platform announcement goes out. Teachers can retain discretion about individual assignments and still have questions about the shared defaults, which student groups get access and how the school describes the service to families. [UC Office of the President, 19 November](https://www.universityofcalifornia.edu/press-room/uc-expands-collaboration-openai-offering-free-access-students-faculty-and-staff-secure) · [UC Academic Council, 20 November](https://senate.universityofcalifornia.edu/_files/reports/council-chair-to-president-open-ai-decision.pdf).

- **A Thai virtual-reality pilot ended at a real computer bench.** Puttida Sakulviriyakitkul developed a hand-tracked Meta Quest 3 simulation for trainee computer teachers in Bangkok who had limited access to current hardware. The sequence included website lessons, virtual practice, a session on physical computers and a debrief. The 35 trainees with matched knowledge tests went from 8.20 to 9.09 out of 10 on average. Thirty-six trainees' end-of-sequence hardware performance averaged 4.85 out of five in the instructor's ratings. The tests had a ceiling effect, the instructor who designed the simulation was the sole practical assessor, and the single-group design leaves the effect of the headset unresolved. Generative AI helped the author produce media for the simulation. The useful choice for a school maker programme is that learners ultimately had to perform on the equipment they were training to use. For a workshop short of kits, that suggests a practical split. Rehearsal can take place away from the bench, and bench time can be spent assembling, testing and explaining an actual fault. [Journal of Education and Training Studies, 19 November](https://redfame.com/journal/index.php/jets/article/download/9389/7538).

## Sceptic's corner

An institution can make a defensible case for providing managed AI access even when teachers disagree on classroom use. The UC president's case is concrete. Students and staff were already using personal accounts outside UC's negotiated terms, and wider managed access could improve privacy and affordability. There is a cost to waiting if the existing pattern persists. There is also a cost to treating the licence as the moment when the consequential choices have already been made. The UC Academic Council's request for consultation before local deployment puts that second point on paper. [UC correspondence, 19 and 20 November](https://senate.universityofcalifornia.edu/_files/reports/council-chair-to-president-open-ai-decision.pdf).

Consider an English learner using a school-provided tool to rehearse an answer. The teacher might welcome vocabulary suggestions during practice and still require a fresh unaided response at the end of a unit. Both parts are coherent if students get the distinction before the task, understand which feedback they are expected to act on and see the same guidance in the assignment and on the platform. The arrangement could leave one student applying automated rewrites to every piece of work just because the tool stays visible. Course-level discretion is easiest to protect when staff can influence how the tools first appear to students. A licence can settle the account question on a particular date. Teachers still have to settle what an accessible tool is for, one task at a time.
