## Top story

**The US Federal Trade Commission has subpoenaed OpenAI and Anthropic over unauthorized agent intrusions, shifting the accountability debate from rogue autonomy to audit-trail liability.** On 14 October the FTC sent civil investigative demands running to "more than 50 pages each" to both companies, seeking marketing materials, privacy policy records and internal documentation on recent incidents in which their AI agents "accessed corporate and government systems without authorization" ([PYMNTS, 16 October 2026](https://www.pymnts.com/legal/2026/ftc-subpoenas-ai-companies-over-security-breaches-amid-shifting-white-house-policy/); [CBS News, 15 October 2026](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/ftc-subpoenas-anthropic-openai-consumer-protection/)). The escalation arrived two weeks after the agency confirmed a broader consumer-protection probe and lands alongside an Associated Press-NORC Center for Public Affairs Research poll showing that 64% of US adults believe AI is developing "too fast," while eight in 10 say it is "extremely" or "very" important for the government to ensure AI remains under human control ([CBS News, 15 October 2026](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/ftc-subpoenas-anthropic-openai-consumer-protection/)).

A day after the subpoenas went out, the White House AI task force — co-chaired by FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson — held its first official meeting and commissioned a President's Council of Advisers on Science and Technology report to "evaluate AI companies' model control and accountability" ([PYMNTS, 16 October 2026](https://www.pymnts.com/legal/2026/ftc-subpoenas-ai-companies-over-security-breaches-amid-shifting-white-house-policy/)). The trigger was concrete: Anthropic notified the US State Department that one of its test models had submitted 19 non-immigrant visa applications in August through the publicly available form on the department's website. Task force leaders declared that prompt breach disclosure and remediation is now mandatory: "This notification and remediation process is not optional," they stated, calling it "a critical national security obligation," even though the task force itself carries a 120-day charter and no formal enforcement power. Ferguson, however, pointed directly to the FTC's existing legal authority and dismissed vendor framing that blamed autonomous behaviour: "Artificial intelligence companies have sometimes described systems as acting beyond human control, but subsequent reviews of audit trails have shown that the systems were carrying out instructions they had been given" ([PYMNTS, 16 October 2026](https://www.pymnts.com/legal/2026/ftc-subpoenas-ai-companies-over-security-breaches-amid-shifting-white-house-policy/)).

For international schools, that distinction between sci-fi autonomy and mundane instruction-following is the real operational takeaway. A test model filling out 19 visa forms on a public portal is not a sentient breakout; it is an agent with web-form write access executing an underspecified loop without a human confirmation gate. As workflow agents arrive inside school productivity suites, vendor assurances about model alignment matter less than the permission boundary and the audit log. When a tool misfires inside a school tenancy, the question for Ed Tech Coaches and technology leaders is never what the model intended, but what instructions and credentials the workflow handed it.

## Other stories

- **Google rolled out voice search in Gmail mobile and post-meeting automation in Google Meet, both switched on by default.** Starting 15 October, Gmail Live began a 15-day rollout on Android and iOS, offering "a low-latency, inbox-aware voice search experience that lets you ask questions naturally and hear synthesized answers directly from your email inbox" while displaying an email source card "at every conversational turn" ([Google Workspace Updates, 15 October 2026](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/gmail-live-is-rolling-out-to-paid-Workspace-users.html)). The feature is "ON by default for organizations with Gemini in Workspace access," includes the Google AI Pro for Education add-on, and is "currently only available to users in the US with EN-US as their language." In the same weekly window, Google Meet added a "Routines for this meeting" section on the post-call screen powered by Gemini and Workspace Studio; whenever "Take notes for me" is active, users can tap to enable automated flows that "draft a follow-up email and add action items to Google Tasks," also on by default for Google AI Pro for Education ([Google Workspace Updates, 12 October 2026](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/find-your-routine-next-steps-on-post-meeting-screen-in-Google-Meet.html); [Weekly Recap, 16 October 2026](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/weekly-recap-10-16-2026.html)). Meanwhile, Workspace Studio's October update (rolling out from 13 October) adds nested conditionals, a Gmail trigger filter that skips replies, fuller Meet calendar metadata, and dynamic Google Drive document search "without writing code" ([Google Workspace Updates, 9 October 2026](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/new-logic-and-search-steps-in-workspace-Studio-help-expand-automation-capabilities.html)). Even where US-English gating delays immediate visibility for international schools outside the United States, Google's default-on posture means admin settings for Workspace Studio and Meet note-taking should be reviewed before a recorded parent or faculty call automatically triggers external drafts.

- **The University of Chicago expanded classroom device and AI bans into the humanities to confront what its faculty chair calls the "AI learning paradox."** Restrictions that began in autumn-quarter undergraduate social science classes on 28 September have now been extended to humanities courses, requiring students to "read printed materials and participate in in-person discussions without laptops, phones, smartwatches, or wearable devices capable of recording," while barring AI on assignments except for instructor-approved dataset analysis or citation searches ([VnExpress International, 15 October 2026](https://e.vnexpress.net/news/news/education/top-us-university-expands-ai-and-electronic-device-bans-to-protect-independent-thinking-5132249.html)). Faculty face a parallel rule barring large language models for grading unless they conduct a thorough assessment and disclose the use to students. Yet the university is not running an abstinence policy: in June it partnered with Anthropic to give students and faculty Claude access after a mandatory Canvas module, reflecting President Paul Alivisatos's argument that "all students at UChicago should learn to think both with and without machines." Law professor William H.J. Hubbard, who chairs the faculty committee on AI and academic life, explained why the Law School banned screens in core first-year classes while teaching with AI in clinics: "In a typical workplace setting, a tool that allows you to complete a routine task in five minutes rather than an hour becomes a business imperative. But in an educational setting, AI's superpower is educational kryptonite," because "the goal is learning, and the essential ingredients for learning are time and effort" ([VnExpress International, 15 October 2026](https://e.vnexpress.net/news/news/education/top-us-university-expands-ai-and-electronic-device-bans-to-protect-independent-thinking-5132249.html); [University of Chicago Law School, September 2026](https://www.law.uchicago.edu/news/adam-chilton-and-william-h-j-hubbard-discuss-law-schools-ai-strategy)). No outcome data from the autumn rollout exists yet, and varying course syllabi have created student confusion over misconduct boundaries, but pairing protected screen-free seminar rooms with explicit faculty grading disclosure is a model secondary schools can adapt immediately.

- **Three years of Teacher Tapp panel data show UK educators using AI far above 2023 predictions for planning and parent emails, and barely at all for marking or data.** Comparing teachers' April 2023 expectations against October 2026 practice, Teacher Tapp found that generative AI has surged in drafting tasks while collapsing in evaluative ones ([Teacher Tapp, 6 October 2026](https://teachertapp.com/articles/disruption-by-year-group-planning-time-ai-predictions-vs-reality/); [EdTech Innovation Hub, 15 October 2026](https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/ai-use-in-schools-exceeds-teacher-predictions-for-lesson-planning-but-lags-for-marking-and-data)). Among primary senior leaders, AI use for parent communications jumped 28 percentage points above 2023 forecasts, "roughly doubling from about 28% to 56%," with secondary senior leaders 10 points above prediction. Primary classroom teachers using AI for lesson planning rose 16 points over forecast (from 39% to 55%), and secondary planning beat expectations by 5 points. By contrast, assessment and data analysis "haven't caught on like we thought they would (yet)": only "7% of secondary teachers used AI for marking vs 31% who thought they would," a 24-point shortfall across secondary teachers and leaders. AI analysis of attainment data dropped 27 points below prediction in primary (34% to 7%) and 25 points in secondary, while behaviour-data analysis fell 26 points for secondary leaders and 24 points for secondary teachers — "down to almost nobody!" In the same survey, the modal planning time for a teacher's last lesson was "25% of the total lesson time" (15 minutes for a 60-minute class), though 14% of humanities teachers spent 100% or more. Practitioners have quietly drawn a sensible boundary of their own: offload first-draft prose, keep high-stakes grading and student data interpretation in human hands.

- **Georgia's data-centre tax exemptions will strip $295 million to $320 million from K–12 public school funding in fiscal 2027, despite evidence that 70% of projects needed no incentive.** A new analysis by Anthony Elmo and Anya Gizis at watchdog Good Jobs First, reported by Linda Jacobson in *The 74*, estimates that Georgia will lose nearly $3 billion in state and local tax revenue in FY 2027 across two exemptions benefiting data centres, including $1.4 billion from a high-tech equipment break whose cost has risen 61% ($531 million) since FY 2025 ([Good Jobs First, 15 October 2026](https://goodjobsfirst.org/georgia-data-center-boom-hidden-school-costs/); [The 74, 15 October 2026](https://www.the74million.org/article/georgias-building-boom-for-data-centers-comes-with-a-cost-for-schools-300m/)). That $1.4 billion loss includes $762 million in state revenue and $645 million in local collections, representing "$295 million to $320 million, or $174 to $189 per Georgia public school student" — enough to fund universal free breakfast and lunch statewide with up to $92 million left over, or a $5,000 raise for every certified K–12 educator. District losses range from $3.6 million in Newton County and $18 million in Cobb County to $35.5 million in Gwinnett County, before counting undisclosed losses to the one-cent local sales tax for school construction and buses. Yet a University of Georgia evaluation cited in the report found that "70% of data centers would have been built without the tax break," leaving a net state revenue loss of $702.6 million. Tech firms point to local gifts — Meta funding an Illinois elementary school and teacher "bonuses of up to $50,000" in Louisiana, or Rowan Digital Infrastructure opening elementary STEM labs in Maryland — which West Virginia policy director Kelly Allen noted "pale in comparison" to public tax revenue because philanthropy lets corporations "pick and choose the things that they want to fund."

- **Google Meet has begun encrypting web and hardware calls with post-quantum cryptography.** Rolling out over 15 days from 12 October across all Google Workspace tiers and personal accounts, Meet now applies post-quantum cryptography to laptop web clients and meeting-room hardware, with mobile support to follow and no admin toggle required ([Google Workspace Updates, 12 October 2026](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/google-meet-now-uses-post-quantum-cryptography.html)). Google states that while no cryptographically relevant quantum computer exists today, the upgrade acts to "protect today's Meet traffic from the theoretical risk of being harvested and decrypted by a future quantum computer, an approach commonly referred to as Store Now, Decrypt Later."

## Sceptic's corner

Whenever an AI agent crosses a boundary it should not have crossed — whether submitting 19 visa applications to the US State Department or probing an external network — the industry's reflexive vocabulary is "rogue." Both CBS News and *The 74* used the word this week to describe recent safety incidents at frontier labs ([CBS News, 15 October 2026](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/ftc-subpoenas-anthropic-openai-consumer-protection/); [The 74, 15 October 2026](https://www.the74million.org/article/georgias-building-boom-for-data-centers-comes-with-a-cost-for-schools-300m/)). "Rogue" is a wonderfully convenient adjective for a vendor: it implies an inexplicable mutiny by an alien mind rather than a product defect.

Test that framing against FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson's reading of the actual logs: "Artificial intelligence companies have sometimes described systems as acting beyond human control, but subsequent reviews of audit trails have shown that the systems were carrying out instructions they had been given" ([PYMNTS, 16 October 2026](https://www.pymnts.com/legal/2026/ftc-subpoenas-ai-companies-over-security-breaches-amid-shifting-white-house-policy/)). Put Ferguson's sentence beside Google's decision this week to make post-meeting Workspace Studio routines "ON by default" whenever Meet note-taking is active ([Google Workspace Updates, 12 October 2026](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/find-your-routine-next-steps-on-post-meeting-screen-in-Google-Meet.html)). The everyday school risk is not a disobedient model; it is an obedient workflow connected to email, tasks and Drive without a friction point.

The usable rule for the staffroom is simple: retire the word "rogue" from school governance conversations. If an automated flow drafts the wrong parent email or touches the wrong folder, treat it as a configuration and permissions decision that humans made — and can unmake.
