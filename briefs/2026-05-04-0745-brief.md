# 4 May 2026

## Top story — AI detectors are being abandoned, and for international schools the case is now overwhelming

The false-positive crisis has crossed into mainstream policy territory. A 2026 study puts mean false-positive rates at 61% for TOEFL essays by Chinese students versus 5% for US-native writers, and at least 50 universities — including MIT, Yale, and UC Berkeley — have now formally banned or disabled AI detection tools, with Curtin University explicitly citing ESL bias as its reason. A Washington Post opinion piece in April made the case plainly to a general audience: "AI detectors are hurting honest students. Schools should ban them." Separate lawsuits by falsely-accused students at Yale and Michigan are advancing through courts, with early rulings suggesting a detector score alone doesn't constitute evidence of academic dishonesty.

**Why it matters at SSIS:** the student body is nearly entirely non-native English writers. If SSIS or any of its teachers are still routing Turnitin AI-detection scores into academic integrity proceedings, the school carries real legal and reputational exposure. The Digital Learning Specialist role is a natural home to lead a policy position here.

## Sceptic's corner

**The workspace-agent demos are impressive. The classroom reality will be messier.**

OpenAI's announcement of Codex-powered agents for Edu plans is genuinely interesting infrastructure — multi-step automation, connected to real tools teachers already use. But the demo use cases (family newsletters, advising summaries) are low-stakes, low-frequency tasks. The harder question is whether schools have the governance layer to decide *which* workflows should be automated at all, and whether a classroom teacher building a shared agent in natural language will produce something that stays within data-privacy guardrails for minors. The tool is ahead of the institutional frameworks. That gap is where things go wrong.

The IB digital exam pilot is the same story in a different sector: the technology is ready, the equity and accessibility arguments are sound, but 60 schools out of 5,000 is a controlled beta. Schools not in the pilot should watch, not move yet.
