# Thursday, 28 May 2026

## Top story

**Microsoft has put a student-study agent inside the managed school tenant. That is a useful category marker.**

Microsoft has made its student-facing **Study and Learn** agent generally available inside Microsoft 365 Copilot for education customers, at no additional cost, for students 13 and older once IT enables it. The important signal is the managed-school shape: student AI inside the institution's existing environment, with age controls, administrative enablement, and a learning-science frame around scaffolding, retrieval practice, productive struggle, and active learning. ([Microsoft Education](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/education/blog/2026/05/study-and-learn-ai-built-for-your-student/))

For international schools, this is less a Microsoft-only story than a category marker. The question is shifting from "Should students use AI?" to "Which governed, school-owned student-AI experiences are responsible enough to enable?" Gemini, NotebookLM, AI Studio, Microsoft Copilot, and LMS assistants need to be judged by that institutional standard, not only against the unmanaged use happening in personal accounts.

The practical comparison is a ladder: personal chatbot, school-managed chatbot, course-grounded notebook, LMS-integrated assistant, teacher-authored AI learning activity. That framing lets schools discuss learning design and governance level at the same time.

## Sceptic's corner

The phrase to squeeze today is **"AI tutor."**

It is wonderfully convenient. Parents like it because tutoring sounds supportive. Vendors like it because tutoring sounds pedagogical. Schools like it because tutoring sounds safer than "answer machine." The trouble is that a tutor is not defined by being available at midnight with a friendly tone. A tutor has to know what the learner is trying to learn, when to withhold help, when to push, when to correct, and when to send the student back to the teacher, the text, or the scratch paper.

Microsoft's Study and Learn at least names productive struggle and scaffolding. Khan Academy is at least publishing measures that point toward cognitive engagement. Good. Better than the usual confetti. But the school test remains local and dull: can the tool sit inside a teacher-designed routine where the learning goal is visible, the allowed support is explicit, and the student still has work that belongs to them?

If the answer is yes, call it a study agent and use it carefully. If the answer is no, do not let the word tutor launder the same old shortcut. A chatbot wearing elbow patches is still a chatbot.
