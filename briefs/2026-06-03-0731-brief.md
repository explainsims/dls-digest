# Wednesday, 3 June 2026

## Top story

**The AI procurement question is moving from vendor claims to independent evidence.** Boston University's Wheelock College, Just Horizons Alliance, and BU's Center for Computing & Data Sciences have launched the AI Ethics Index for K-12 Education, a three-year effort to evaluate the ethical dimensions and real-world impact of AI systems used in schools. The project is explicitly aimed at educators, policymakers, funders, and companies who need better evidence than broad assurances when judging AI products ([Boston University Wheelock](https://www.bu.edu/wheelock/news/articles/2026/new-ai-ethics-index-launches/)).

For international schools and Ed Tech Coaches, the practical read is straightforward: procurement needs another layer. A privacy policy and a vendor demo are not enough. The next serious review question is: what independent evidence exists for safety, learning value, data handling, bias, developmental impact, and real school use? This fits the broader shift in AI governance from acceptable-use documents toward adoption gates, rubrics, review records, and retirement rules that can survive parent, leadership, and audit questions.

## Other stories

- **CoSN's 2026 State of EdTech report gives schools a useful benchmark language.** More than 600 U.S. K-12 technology leaders were surveyed; cybersecurity and data privacy remain the top priorities, nearly 80% report having AI guidelines, and 55% require vendors to provide product-safety information before adoption. The weakness is just as useful: fewer districts ask for evidence-based design, inclusivity, usability, or interoperability data, and 58% say they are understaffed for instructional technology support ([CoSN](https://www.cosn.org/cosn-news/u-s-state-of-edtech-report-examines-how-k-12-districts-are-using-technology-to-support-teaching-and-learning/)). That is a clean reminder that AI policy maturity and classroom implementation capacity are not the same thing.

- **Google has made Gemini LTI and Assignments LTI available for Moodle.** Gemini LTI brings Gemini and NotebookLM into Moodle, following the same direction of travel seen with Schoology: AI support is moving into LMS context rather than sitting outside the course shell ([Google Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/05/workspace-assignments-lti-and-gemini-lti-for-moodle.html)). The useful question is no longer simply "Which chatbot can a teacher use?" It is "Which LMS-connected AI workflows are available, which data do they touch, and where does teacher review sit?"

- **The UK AI tutoring programme is now a useful safety-infrastructure case study.** The Department for Education and DSIT are inviting companies to build classroom-ready AI tutoring tools for disadvantaged pupils, with teacher supervision, curriculum alignment, evidence of impact, safety standards, benchmark development, and pupil-data limits in the programme language ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/edtech-and-ai-companies-invited-to-help-build-safe-ai-tutoring-tools-for-disadvantaged-pupils)). The associated generative-AI product safety standards were updated in January 2026 to include cognitive development, social and emotional development, mental health, and manipulation ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/generative-ai-product-safety-expectations)).

## Sceptic's corner

The risk now is governance theatre. Schools can look mature because they have AI guidelines, a committee, a vendor checklist, and a few approved tools, while the actual classroom layer remains under-supported. CoSN's numbers show the contradiction neatly: AI guidelines are spreading, but instructional technology staffing is still thin, and procurement routines often stop at product safety rather than evidence of learning design, accessibility, interoperability, and measurable impact.

The stronger version of AI adoption will be slower and less glamorous. It will ask teachers to redesign tasks, ask vendors for uncomfortable evidence, ask students to disclose process rather than merely submit products, and ask leaders to fund instructional support rather than only platform access. If AI becomes embedded into Google Classroom, Moodle, Schoology, tutoring systems, and assessment workflows, the school's policy target is no longer "AI use." It is the entire learning infrastructure. That is a much harder object to govern.
