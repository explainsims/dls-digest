# Tuesday, 2 June 2026

## Top story

**Google's most useful AI update this week is infrastructure, not novelty.** Google Classroom is rolling out learning goals: educators can tag assignments, questions, materials, quiz assignments, and rubrics with standards or skills, then use analytics to see student performance against those goals. AI can suggest relevant goals from assignment and course content, while standards are drawn through 1EdTech / CASE-compatible sources. The rollout began 27 May and is available across Google Workspace for Education Fundamentals, Standard, and Plus.

For Ed Tech Coaches and international schools, this is a useful shift in the AI conversation. The question is no longer just whether teachers can generate lesson plans or quizzes. It is whether the learning platform can connect curriculum goals, teacher-designed tasks, student evidence, and instructional decisions in a way that teachers can actually use. The caution: standards coverage is still uneven across countries and curriculum systems, so schools should verify local framework support before treating this as a ready-made standards solution.

## Other stories

- **Gemini-assisted Drive organization is now generally available.** Google's "Organize My Files" can suggest where loose Drive files should move, propose new folders, let users review choices, and warn about permission changes before files are moved. For schools, this is a practical workflow signal: AI support is moving into everyday resource management, not just classroom-facing prompts.

- **NotebookLM now syncs Drive sources automatically.** Google says NotebookLM can keep Docs, Sheets, and Slides sources up to date as the Drive files change, while respecting deleted files and revoked permissions. This strengthens one of the safest school AI patterns: source-grounded notebooks based on teacher-selected materials.

- **Schoology gains a tighter Gemini LTI / NotebookLM path.** Gemini LTI users in PowerSchool Schoology can now add course content directly as NotebookLM sources. This matters because learning-assistant workflows are moving closer to the LMS and course-material layer, where schools can govern access and context more clearly.

- **An AI Ethics Index for K-12 tools is being built.** Boston University and Just Horizons Alliance announced a three-year project to evaluate AI tools marketed to children and schools, including mental health effects, privacy-law compliance, and other safety dimensions. Schools should watch this because procurement needs independent evidence, not only vendor claims.

- **A phased district rollout offers a useful pattern.** Acton-Boxborough in Massachusetts is moving from working group and readiness assessment, to limited high-school Gemini use at teacher discretion in 2026-27, then broader expansion later. The sequence is sensible: readiness, professional learning, limited use, review, then scale.

## On the radar

- **ISTELive / ASCD in late June** is shaping up as a major AI implementation checkpoint, with sessions on AI + PBL, evidence, product impact, and AI-ready graduates.

- **The teacher-guidance gap remains the background issue.** Gallup/Walton reported that only 18% of U.S. K-12 teachers had formal administrative guidance for AI use at work. More embedded AI features make clear local guidance more important, not less.

## Sceptic's corner

AI inside Classroom, Drive, NotebookLM, and the LMS can make adoption feel mature before the school has made the hard decisions. The tool layer is improving quickly. The governance layer still has to answer: what counts as evidence of learning, what requires disclosure, which data can be used, when students may interact with AI directly, and where human judgment is non-negotiable.
