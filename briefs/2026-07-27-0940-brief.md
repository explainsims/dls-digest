## Top story

**Anthropic put frontier-class capability on the ordinary price tier and turned the reasoning budget into a dial.** Claude Opus 5 shipped on 24 July "priced at $5 per million input tokens and $25 per million output tokens (the same as Opus 4.8)", with an adjustable effort setting running from a lowest rung up through high, xhigh and max. Anthropic calls it "the new state-of-the-art" on the Frontier-Bench and GDPval-AA evaluations, and says it surpasses "Fable 5's best result at just over a third of the cost" on the OSWorld 2.0 computer-use benchmark ([Anthropic, 24 July 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5)). It is the fourth Claude 5 model in under two months; Mythos 5, Fable 5 and Sonnet 5 all landed in June ([TechCrunch, 24 July 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/24/anthropic-launches-opus-5/)).

The dial deserves more attention than the leaderboard. Until now the honest answer to per-call cost in a school-built tool was: use the weaker model, accept worse output. An effort setting on a strong model replaces that binary with something tunable per task, and the CursorBench 3.2 figure is the clearest illustration: at max effort Opus 5 lands "within 0.5% of Fable 5's peak score, but at half the cost per task." That is a budgeting change dressed up as a parameter, and it matters most to schools that are building their own small tools.

Two cautions. Every figure above is Anthropic's own, none of it independently reproduced. And four flagship releases in under two months means any staff training built around a named model is stale by half-term; teach the task and the judgement, not the model.

## Other stories

- **The largest open-weight model ever built released its weights this morning, and effectively nobody outside a data centre can run them.** Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 — 2.8 trillion parameters in a mixture-of-experts design that fires 16 of 896 experts per token, about 50 billion parameters of live compute — was slated to publish full weights on 27 July, after ranking third on Artificial Analysis's intelligence index behind only Claude Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol Max, and first in the Frontend Code Arena ([Interconnects, 20 July 2026](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/kimi-k3-the-open-weights-escalation)). In MXFP4 the weights come to roughly 1.4 TB, which puts self-hosting within reach of an eight-node cluster of 80GB GPUs and nowhere near any school's server room ([TechTimes, 25 July 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/321551/20260725/kimi-k3-open-weights-arrive-sunday-self-hosting-cuts-china-data-risk-api-never-can.htm)). The school-visible effect is second-order: open weights this strong drag hosted inference prices down, which is where a school actually feels it.

- **Google is rebuilding the Google Classroom homepage for everyone, starting today, with no admin control over it.** Announced 21 July and rolling out from 27 July to all editions globally — Workspace customers, Individual subscribers and personal accounts alike — the redesign gives teachers a dashboard of assignment completion and student interaction, students an "Enrolled" view of what is due, and admins usage analytics, with collapsible modules and role switching for people who are both. Google states plainly that no admin controls govern the new homepage, and that Gemini modules appear only where Gemini is already enabled for the org unit ([Google Workspace Updates, 21 July 2026](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/07/redesigned-google-classroom-homepage-with-tailored-views-based-on-users-role.html)). In the many international schools where Classroom is a secondary surface rather than the main one, the point is not the dashboard but the visibility: something most staff treat as peripheral is about to look like a system of record to students.

- **A quarter of the teenagers who have seen AI sexual imagery say it depicted them or someone they know, and school is where they are least likely to raise it.** Common Sense Media's Youth AI Safety Institute, with Burson, surveyed 1,314 US teenagers aged 13–17 and found 44% believe they have seen AI-generated sexual content; of the 586 who had, a quarter said it showed them or someone they knew, 79% came across it accidentally, and only 13% felt comfortable telling a teacher or counsellor — while 44% said school had taught them nothing about identifying AI-generated material ([Common Sense Media, 21 July 2026](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/research/teens-and-explicit-deepfakes-in-the-age-of-ai)). The report's school recommendations are unusually concrete: set a response protocol before an incident, distinguish targets from bystanders from creators from sharers, and verify that approved AI tools cannot generate intimate imagery at all.

- **UNESCO has turned its global AI ethics framework into a free Coursera course.** The Global MOOC on the Ethics of AI, co-developed with LG AI Research and drawing on Carnegie Mellon, the University of Toronto and the Alan Turing Institute, runs ten modules across safety, fairness, privacy, data governance, transparency, accountability, environmental cost, human autonomy and global governance; it is free, self-paced, available in eleven languages, and completable in about a week with a shareable certificate ([EdTech Innovation Hub, 24 July 2026](https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/unesco-turns-global-ai-ethics-framework-into-free-coursera-course)). It is aimed at university students, researchers and policymakers rather than classroom teachers, but it is still the cheapest credible AI-ethics training on the table — a week of self-paced study with an institutional name attached, which is more than most vendor courses offer.

- **ChatGPT Voice now drives agents rather than just conversation, and it is on the Edu tier.** OpenAI shipped Voice in Work and Codex to the desktop app on 23 July, letting users direct tasks, coordinate multiple agents and keep background work moving by speaking, alongside a real-time Voice in Chat; it rolled out to Plus, Pro, Business, Edu and Enterprise plans, metered at roughly five to six credits per minute on flexible-pricing workspaces ([OpenAI, 23 July 2026](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10128477-chatgpt-enterprise-edu-release-notes)). Voice as an input to a chatbot was a convenience; voice as a controller for agents that touch files and repositories is a permissions question, and it has arrived on the education tier without a separate governance story attached.

## Sceptic's corner

The claim to test this week: open weights buy sovereignty. TechTimes ran it as a headline on 25 July — self-hosting Kimi K3 "cuts China data risk the API never can" ([TechTimes, 25 July 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/321551/20260725/kimi-k3-open-weights-arrive-sunday-self-hosting-cuts-china-data-risk-api-never-can.htm)). The logic looks clean: download the weights, run them on your own metal, nothing leaves the building.

Now test it against what a school can actually do. The weights run to about 1.4 TB and want eight nodes of 80GB GPUs before there is room for a KV cache. No school, and very few universities, will host this. What schools will get is the same model served by whoever can afford the cluster — which is a different vendor relationship, not the absence of one. Sovereignty has been relocated, not acquired.

There is a second gap. "Open weights" here means downloadable, not inspectable: no training data, no alignment corpus, no stated licence at announcement. You can audit what the model does; you cannot audit what shaped it. And the risk that actually walks into a school this term is the one in the deepfake research above — students generating intimate imagery of classmates — which no weight custody touches.

The usable line: open weights are a pricing event and a research gift, not a data-protection control. Treating them as one swaps a hardware fantasy for a policy you still have to write.
