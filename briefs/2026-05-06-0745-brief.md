# 6 May 2026

## Top story — NYC's AI politics flipped this week, and the cover story for slow-rolling AI in schools just got a lot stronger

The NYC DOE feedback window was supposed to close quietly on Friday 8 May with refinements baked into the June Playbook. Instead, two things happened in the 48 hours before that deadline.

First, on 1 May, more than 100 parents and students testified through a seven-hour Panel for Educational Policy meeting demanding a two-year AI moratorium. The headline detail: Naveed Hasan — the panel's de-facto tech expert, who had previously *supported* the AI-themed high school — reversed and endorsed the moratorium, citing data-privacy infrastructure and learning concerns. ([Chalkbeat](https://www.chalkbeat.org/newyork/2026/05/01/parents-demand-ai-moratorium-in-schools-during-marathon-panel-for-educational-policy-meeting/), [The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/nyc-parents-and-students-demand-moratorium-at-marathon-meeting/))

Second, on 4 May, the NY State Comptroller dropped an audit that found NYC schools had **141 data breaches between 2023-25**, that the DOE delayed reporting 48% of them to the state and 11% to families (delays of up to 460 days), and that the system "does not have a clear understanding" of what data it collects. The DOE accepted most recommendations. ([Chalkbeat](https://www.chalkbeat.org/newyork/2026/05/04/state-comptroller-audit-finds-student-data-privacy-gaps-in-nyc-schools/), [Route Fifty](https://www.route-fifty.com/management/2026/05/state-audit-slams-nyc-schools-lack-student-data-privacy-oversight/413326/))

**Why this matters for international schools:** the NYC story has been the reference point everyone in international ed-tech circles cites when they say "the leading public system is moving forward, so we should too." That framing just lost altitude. When a panel's own technologist flips to moratorium and a state auditor calls the data hygiene rotten in the same week, the optics for any school still in *planning* phase get easier — slow and careful is no longer the laggard position. The defensible play is teacher-facing tooling first, governance and data-classification work in parallel, student-facing rollout last and small. NYC just demonstrated what skipping the middle step looks like.

The June Playbook will still drop, and the May 8 feedback window still closes Friday. Worth checking what the DOE actually folds in — but the headline finding is now political, not curricular.

## Sceptic's corner

**Don't read NYC as proof the parents are right — read it as proof the politics are real.**

It's tempting, especially after a state audit and a moratorium endorsement, to treat the NYC backlash as vindication of the precautionary case. That's a mis-read. The audit findings (delayed breach reporting, no software inventory) are about *governance failure*, not about AI per se — those would damn any tech rollout, AI or not. And the moratorium movement is genuinely mixed: some of it is substantive privacy work from groups like the Parent Coalition for Student Privacy; some is the broader anti-tech-in-schools current that would oppose Chromebooks too.

The useful signal isn't "AI in schools is bad." It's **"any AI rollout that doesn't lead with data governance loses the political argument the moment something goes wrong."** NYC didn't lose this fight on pedagogy — it lost on inventory and breach notifications. The precise lesson for international schools: the data-classification, vendor-inventory, and breach-protocol work is the *load-bearing* part of an AI plan. The pedagogy and tooling work sits on top of it. Skip the foundation, and you don't get to deploy anything for a year while you fix it under pressure.
