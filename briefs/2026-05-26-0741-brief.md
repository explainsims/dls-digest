# Tuesday, 26 May 2026

## Top story

**The agent layer is moving into the school office suite. Governance has to move from app approval to action approval.**

Google's I/O week did not stop at model announcements. The sharper school signal is that Gemini is being framed as a cross-app actor: Workspace updates name voice workflows in Gmail, Docs, and Keep, AI Inbox, Google Pics, and Gemini Spark, a 24/7 personal AI agent that can integrate with Workspace apps and act on a user's behalf under direction. Google says Spark will be available soon in preview for Workspace business customers, while the Gemini app version can gather urgent Gmail updates, track Calendar events, and suggest next steps. ([Google Workspace](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/workspace/workspace-updates/), [Google Gemini app](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/next-evolution-gemini-app/))

The education relevance is indirect but not distant. Schools do not need Gemini Spark in Education tomorrow for the pattern to matter. Google has already made Workspace Intelligence available to Education Plus, Google AI Pro for Education, and Teaching and Learning add-on customers. That layer grounds generative AI tasks in Gmail, Chat, Calendar, Drive, Docs, Sheets, and Slides, is on by default where available, and gives admins data-source controls by domain, OU, or group. Crucially, Google's admin note says that disabling a data source stops active search across that source, but users can still ask Gemini about a specific file they provide. ([Google Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/04/introducing-workspace-intelligence-with-admin-controls.html))

For international schools, this is the next useful control-plane question. The old approval unit was "Can teachers use this app?" The new unit is "What actions may AI take, against which data, inside which account boundary, with what review?" A Gmail summary is one thing. A Calendar-aware daily brief is another. A NotebookLM-grounded Workspace Studio flow is another again. A future agent that drafts, schedules, routes, or updates records is not just a fancier chatbot; it is a delegated workflow.

For Ed Tech Coaches and school technology leaders, this argues for a school AI control matrix before the August sparkle tour. Put rows around actions, not products: summarize, draft, classify, translate, generate feedback, query a source notebook, update a spreadsheet, send a message, create a calendar event, call an external tool. Then mark allowed data classes, approved platforms, age bands, review requirements, logging expectations, and "hard no" zones. Otherwise schools will keep debating "AI tools" while the tools quietly become office plumbing.

## Sceptic's corner

The phrase to put under the lamp today is **"under your direction."**

It is the comforting line in almost every agent announcement. The AI acts under your direction. It asks before high-stakes actions. It works with your files, your inbox, your calendar, your workflow. Lovely. Also incomplete.

In schools, "your" is rarely simple. A teacher's Drive can contain student work, shared department material, old drafts of policy, parent communications, assessment accommodations, and exported spreadsheet oddities that nobody remembers owning. A calendar event can imply medical, behavioural, pastoral, or family context. A source notebook can be personal thinking one week and a de facto knowledge base the next. If an agent acts "under your direction" across that material, the school still has to define what the user was allowed to direct in the first place.

Consent is not the same as authority. A tired staff member clicking approve is not a governance model. The useful test is whether the school can say: this role may perform this action on this data class in this system, with this record of what happened, and this human checkpoint before consequence.

If the answer is no, the problem is not that the agent is too powerful. The problem is that the school has delegated before it has named the delegation.
