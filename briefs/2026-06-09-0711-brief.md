## Top story

**WWDC's school story is controllable device modes, not a cleverer Siri.** Apple's June 8 education notes say a new version of Apple's Classroom app on iPadOS 27 and macOS 27 will let teachers lock student devices to one or more specified websites through Guided Browser, with no IT configuration required. Later this year, multi-app mode will let a teacher confine a lesson to an approved set of apps, while macOS 27 adds a more granular Automatic Assessment Configuration environment for secure testing ([Apple Platform Deployment](https://support.apple.com/en-ca/guide/deployment/education-specific-updates-depacd1b21be/1/web/1.0)).

That control layer arrives beside a much more pervasive AI layer. Siri AI is becoming a conversational assistant with personal-context search, online knowledge, cross-app actions, a dedicated app, and writing support nearly anywhere text can be entered ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/os/)). For managed devices, Apple is moving intelligence controls into declarative device management: IT can allow or deny Writing Tools, image generation, app-specific intelligence, external AI services, and Siri functions, although Apple says additional management for Siri AI and Visual Intelligence will arrive in later beta releases ([Apple Platform Deployment](https://support.apple.com/en-ca/guide/deployment/depd638aa061/1/web/1.0)).

For international schools using Apple hardware alongside Google-centred content and collaboration workflows, the device can become the enforcement surface without dictating the instructional platform. A baseline task, an AI-scaffolded task, an open-AI task, and a secure assessment can now become visibly different device states rather than four paragraphs in a policy. The features are still pre-release, and technical controls cannot decide the pedagogy, but the direction is useful: define the learning mode, then make the device behave accordingly.

## Other stories

- **AI is becoming an operating-system capability.** Apple's new Siri AI can search personal context across photos, email, and notes; take actions in apps; draw on online information; and continue conversations through a dedicated app across compatible devices ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/os/)). Schools will increasingly need to govern capabilities and contexts, not merely maintain an approved-chatbot list.

- **Apple's child-safety expansion is substantial, but mainly family-facing.** New controls include selecting a child's available system apps during setup, requiring approval before visiting new websites, age-guided time allowances and schedules, and Communication Safety interventions for nudity, gore, and violence ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/child-safety/)). These may help families, but they do not replace school device management or a school-wide digital-wellbeing approach.

- **The Naviance settlement calendar has moved.** The court-authorised settlement site now lists a July 27 claims deadline and an August 19 final approval hearing, superseding earlier references to June 10 ([official settlement FAQ](https://www.powerschoolnaviancesettlement.com/faq)). The underlying lesson remains: third-party analytics inside trusted school software can become a student-privacy liability, but the next legal checkpoint is August, not tomorrow.

- **The FTC has made data minimisation part of an ed-tech security order.** Its final order against Illuminate Education follows an alleged breach affecting 10.1 million students and requires a comprehensive security programme, deletion of unnecessary data, limits on collection and retention, a public retention schedule, and accurate breach-notification claims ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/06/ftc-gives-final-approval-order-against-illuminate-settling-allegations-it-failed-secure-students)). "We secure the data" is no longer enough; vendors need to justify why they still hold it.

## Sceptic's corner

The tempting claim after WWDC is that device management can now solve the AI-governance problem. Apple can lock a class to approved websites or apps, disable Writing Tools and external intelligence services on supervised devices, and give assessment vendors a more secure Mac environment. Those are real controls, and schools that ignore them will make policy harder to enforce than it needs to be ([Apple education updates](https://support.apple.com/en-ca/guide/deployment/education-specific-updates-depacd1b21be/1/web/1.0), [Apple device-management updates](https://support.apple.com/en-ca/guide/deployment/depd638aa061/1/web/1.0)).

But a switch can only enforce a decision that someone has already made. "Writing Tools off" does not explain which unaided skill the task is measuring. "AI on" does not distinguish a useful hint from wholesale substitution. Managed-device controls also say little about personal phones, home work, or the browser tab a student opens after the locked session ends. The danger is policy by settings pane: schools inherit the categories a vendor exposes and mistake configurability for instructional clarity.

The usable judgment is narrower. Device controls can make a learning mode credible, but they cannot define the learning target. Write the instructional contract first; configure the device second.
