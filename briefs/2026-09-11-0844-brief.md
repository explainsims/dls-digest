## Top story

**Canvas Lite is free at the point of entry and expensive in governance questions.** Instructure announced on 9 September that its replacement for Free-for-Teacher will open worldwide on 30 September. An individual educator can create courses, invite learners, collect submissions, run quizzes and rubrics, use SpeedGrader, message a class and connect tools including Google Drive, Zoom and Microsoft Teams without an institutional licence. The guardrails are unusually concrete for a free product: every teacher must pass a government-ID-and-selfie check through Veriff; students do not. Canvas Lite runs on a separate domain, all data is hosted in the United States, and the service limits each teacher to five active courses, 50 learners per course and 500 MB of storage. It excludes course analytics, attendance, manually created API tokens and staff-assisted troubleshooting ([Instructure, 9 September 2026](https://www.instructure.com/press-release/instructure-announces-canvas-lite-new-free-course-tool-built-educators); [Instructure Community, 9 September 2026](https://community.instructure.com/en/discussion/667092/built-for-individual-educators-built-with-security-in-mind-introducing-canvas-lite)).

That design is safer than an anonymous classroom shell and less governable than an institutional LMS. Identity verification answers who opened the course; it does not give a school central visibility, retention settings, regional hosting, analytics or an API-based exit route. The separate domain also matters: a teacher may be verified as a person without being authorised by the school to create a student environment. The eligibility guidance is internally inconsistent: the press release permits educators whose institution lacks a full licence; the Community guidance says the product is for individual educators and excludes employees of businesses, non-profit organisations and government agencies; an Instructure staff reply on that same page says private, public and non-profit schools are eligible. That unresolved boundary makes the unit of control—and the contract question—more important, not less.

For international schools, this is a live specimen of the shadow-LMS problem. A well-meaning teacher could stand up a plausible course outside the school’s approved learning platform or managed collaboration suite in minutes, attach cloud storage, and invite pupils before anyone has asked where the records sit or how they leave. The right response is not a blocklist reflex. It is a crisp rule: no student roster or identifiable work enters an individually owned course platform without school approval, a data map and an exit plan. Canvas Lite has made the boundary legible. Schools now have to decide whether they will govern it.

## Other stories

- **Windows is turning age into a platform signal rather than asking every app to collect a birthday.** Microsoft’s 8 September preview of the Windows Age API gives developers non-identifying bands—under 10, 10–12, 13–15, 16–17 and 18+—plus separate checks for whether age has been verified and whether the user is a child, minor or adult under regional rules. The company says apps receive the age signal they need rather than a full date of birth, while Microsoft Age Verification stores a verified status once for reuse across Windows experiences; verification is already available in Microsoft storefronts in Singapore, Brazil and Australia ([Microsoft Windows, 8 September 2026](https://blogs.windows.com/windowsexperience/2026/09/08/helping-families-and-educators-support-safer-experiences-and-healthier-habits-on-windows/)). This is a serious architectural answer to age-appropriate AI, but it sits in consumer Microsoft accounts rather than many schools’ managed identity stacks. Watch the pattern, not the platform: schools should prefer coarse, reusable age assertions over scattering pupils’ birth dates across apps, while still asking who verified the signal and whether a child can bypass it with another account.

- **Missouri’s Google agreement makes statewide access the experiment—and local choice the control group.** Google announced on 8 September that nearly 100,000 educators and more than 1.1 million K–12 and higher-education students will receive no-cost access to Gemini for Education, NotebookLM, the Google AI Educator Series and professional certificates in AI, cybersecurity, data analytics and IT support. The partnership also extends Google courses and certificates to all state residents through job centres. Google says education-workspace conversations are not used to train its models and that institutions keep administrative control; crucially, each district, college and university decides whether and how to participate ([Google, 8 September 2026](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/missouri-state-education-partnership/)). The scale is the news, not the familiar feature list. If the state publishes uptake, workload, classroom-use and learning evidence by district, the voluntary structure could reveal more than a compulsory rollout. Until then, the claimed ten-hours-a-week saving remains vendor-linked research, and free licences measure distribution rather than educational value.

- **OpenAI is putting 400-plus Edu seats into journalism schools, where verification is the curriculum rather than a disclaimer.** Newmark J-School at CUNY and Northwestern’s Medill School will offer ChatGPT Edu subscriptions to interested graduate students and faculty during 2026–27, alongside training and collaboration with their journalism-technology centres. OpenAI names public-record analysis, archive search, translation, data-pattern discovery and new storytelling formats as candidate uses, while the programme says students also need to understand limitations and the places where human judgement remains essential. Edu workspaces provide administrative controls and do not use workspace data for model training ([OpenAI, 8 September 2026](https://openai.com/index/supporting-journalism-from-classrooms-to-newsrooms/)). The interesting school-level analogue is not a journalism chatbot. It is an assignment architecture in which sourcing, corroboration, editorial judgement and correction remain visible while AI helps with the intermediate labour. The announcement contains no assessment design, baseline or promised outcome, so the partnership is best treated as a forthcoming case study rather than evidence that access improves reporting.

- **WestEd has launched an AI coach for principals before publishing the evidence that the coach improves leadership.** My School Improvement Partner invites leaders to upload improvement plans, assessment outcomes, meeting notes and other school data, then uses WestEd’s Four Domains for Rapid School Improvement framework to offer daily reflection prompts, structured inquiry and goal tracking. The product inherits a credible practice framework and the organisation’s coaching experience, but the 10 September announcement offers enthusiasm from early testers rather than a study of decision quality, implementation or student outcomes; it also does not set out pricing, data retention, model providers or whether uploaded records are used beyond the individual engagement ([WestEd, 10 September 2026](https://www.wested.org/blog/news-releases/wested-announces-new-ai-powered-school-improvement-coaching-tool-for-principals/)). For school leaders, that makes the tool an interesting demonstration and a poor destination for real plans until its terms are read. Grounding an assistant in good questions does not automatically make it a confidential coach.

- **Google’s newest AI-plan bundle is a reminder that the same interface can cross account boundaries quietly.** On 9 September Google grouped voice drafting and search in Gmail, Docs and Keep, image creation through Google Pics, prompt-built mini-apps in Sheets canvas, and Gemini Spark errands across Chrome and Photos into its paid consumer AI plans; it also renewed a one-year free offer for eligible university students worldwide ([Google, 9 September 2026](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/google-one/fall-2026-ai-plan-updates/)). These are consumer subscription features, not a Google Workspace for Education release, even though the product names are the same ones teachers and students see at school. That distinction belongs in staff guidance: availability in a personal account is not evidence that a feature is enabled, contracted or governed in the school domain. The practical risk is not that teachers cannot tell Gmail from Docs. It is that they may not notice which identity is holding the prompt, source file, generated image or mini-app when the tool looks identical.

## On the radar

- **Canvas gives former Free-for-Teacher users a seven-day migration window.** The old service is due to reopen from 30 September through 6 October so users can export content into Canvas Lite; existing courses will not move automatically ([Instructure Community, 9 September 2026](https://community.instructure.com/en/discussion/667092/built-for-individual-educators-built-with-security-in-mind-introducing-canvas-lite)). Watch whether exports include a complete Common Cartridge, submissions and records, and whether the 500 MB ceiling strands larger courses. Schools with former users should identify them before the window opens rather than discovering an unofficial archive after it closes. This is an exit test disguised as a product launch.

- **Google’s AI Educator Badge-a-thon runs on 19 September.** Announced on 3 September for a fixed 19 September event, the ten-hour virtual programme promises drop-in training on monthly modules covering task automation, Guided Learning, Deep Research and classroom AI quests, with ISTE-aligned badges ([Google, 3 September 2026](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/new-ai-educator-trainings-september-2026/)). It falls one day outside the normal seven-day source window but is time-sensitive now. Watch whether the modules teach account boundaries, source checking and evidence of learning alongside feature fluency, and whether the badges certify judgement or merely completion. The recording and module artefacts will be more useful than the event-day enthusiasm.

- **Windows’ age layer is not fully shipped.** Microsoft says the Age APIs are available to Windows Insiders, will reach all users soon, and that one status-checking method will arrive in a later update; it gives no general-release date ([Microsoft Windows, 8 September 2026](https://blogs.windows.com/windowsexperience/2026/09/08/helping-families-and-educators-support-safer-experiences-and-healthier-habits-on-windows/)). The questions to watch are whether browsers and major AI apps adopt the signal, what happens on shared devices, and how school-managed identities interoperate with a consumer-account trust chain. A privacy-preserving age band only becomes a safeguard when the applications most likely to expose minors actually honour it.

## Sceptic's corner

Free is doing a great deal of work in this morning’s headlines. Canvas Lite removes the institutional licence. Missouri receives Google tools and credentials at no cost. Journalism schools receive hundreds of ChatGPT Edu seats. Eligible university students receive a year of Google’s consumer AI plan. Each offer may be genuinely useful. None is costless.

The first cost is governance labour. Someone still has to verify the account type, read the data terms, decide what may be uploaded, map the exit route, train users and respond when a feature changes. The second is curricular gravity: once teachers and students build materials, habits and credentials inside a platform, later procurement is no longer a clean choice between vendors. The third is evidence. Distribution numbers arrive immediately because vendors can count licences; learning, workload and equity outcomes arrive slowly, if anyone funds the measurement at all.

That does not make free access a trick. It makes it an adoption strategy. Schools should accept useful offers with their eyes open and a clock running: name the owner, define permitted data, preserve portable copies, decide what success would look like, and set a review date before the promotional period ends. The staffroom line is simple: a zero-dollar invoice tells you who is subsidising entry. It does not tell you who will pay for governance, migration or dependence.
