## What Does It All Mean?

Google's position on Search is now plain. AI Overview and AI Mode are "deeply integrated" into it, so a school cannot keep Search and switch them off ([New York Times via The Star, 10 November](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/11/10/senator-tells-google-to-let-us-schools-disable-ai-features-in-search)). Senator Ed Markey's 9 November letter calls that "an ultimatum to effectively force schools to enable generative AI" ([same report](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/11/10/senator-tells-google-to-let-us-schools-disable-ai-features-in-search)). So the Sunday question: if a school cannot remove the AI box from the results page, what is left for it to govern?

First, why the box won't come off. Tracy Pizzo, who spent more than a decade at Google, much of it on education, told NPR the answer may lie in court rather than in classrooms. In September, Judge Amit Mehta dismissed publishers' antitrust suits; Penske Media's tying claim failed because Search and AI Overviews were not plausibly separate products; reports quote the ruling's phrase "an integrated search experience" ([MIXED, 4 October](https://mixed-news.com/en/judge-mehta-dismisses-chegg-penske-ai-overviews-google-antitrust/)). A schools-only off switch, Pizzo argued, would be evidence that the two can be separated. The antitrust experts NPR asked were not entirely convinced, and Google did not answer ([NPR, 13 November](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/13/nx-s1-6029408/google-ai-search-schools)). Treat it as a theory. It does explain why "off by default" stops at the Search bar.

Second, what the box does to reading. Pew tracked the browsing of 900 US adults in March 2025. When an AI summary appeared, they clicked a traditional result on 8% of visits, against 15% without one, and clicked a link inside the summary on 1% ([Pew Research Center, 22 July 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/)). Common Sense Media found that "AI Mode completed 100% of the homework assignments we gave it", and that neither feature can be switched off by parents or schools ([Common Sense Media, July 2026](https://institute.commonsensemedia.org/risk-assessments/google-search)).

Third, what schools have been trying to teach. The Stanford History Education Group found that professional fact-checkers beat historians and undergraduates at judging websites because they read laterally: they leave the page and check the source elsewhere. In a randomised trial across six high schools, six 50-minute lessons made students twice as likely to spot questionable sites, though they still earned only half the available points ([Stanford GSE, 19 April 2022](https://ed.stanford.edu/news/it-doesn-t-take-long-learn-how-spot-misinformation-online-stanford-study-finds)). Google.org funded the study.

Put those together. The habit information literacy builds is leaving the page. The interface that now greets every query is built so that most people don't.

For an international school on Google, that moves the question from the admin console to the task. Whatever a school decides about Gemini for students, every student who searches will meet an Overview. A school's AI-use scale tells students what they may use; it cannot treat Search as an AI-free zone. A research task can ask for the move the Overview skips: name two sources outside it, say what each adds, and mark where the summary was wrong or thin. A ninth-grader who can do that has a skill the box cannot take away.

## Behind You! Behind You!

**Chicago took the argument to Google's door.** About 30 people, mostly parents and educators, rallied outside Google's West Loop office on Wednesday, alongside a protest in Cambridge, Massachusetts ([Chalkbeat via The 74, 14 November](https://the74million.org/article/protests-in-chicago-after-google-warns-schools-about-disabling-ai-feature)). Chicago Public Schools, which has blocked student access to generative AI tools, said it is "actively exploring all options to independently suppress" the Overview, and failing that will look at other search tools. Watch whether a district that size actually leaves.

**Anthropic signed the teachers' unions' privacy standard.** The AFT and its New York affiliate, the UFT, announced on 10 November a legally binding agreement covering Claude for Teachers and Anthropic's existing district agreements ([K-12 Dive, 10 November](https://www.k12dive.com/news/anthropic-enters-student-ai-privacy-agreement-with-teachers-unions/874193/)). Data may be used only for authorised educational purposes, and schools control how long it is kept and when it is deleted. Microsoft's equivalent took effect on 1 November. A school outside the unions' reach can still ask any vendor to match those terms in writing.

**Ho Chi Minh City began planning its AI periods.** The city's Department of Education and Training held a workshop on 12 November at Le Hong Phong High School for the Gifted on bringing the national AI education framework into secondary schools this year: 12 periods per class per school year, as topics or woven into subjects ([SGGP, 12 November](https://en.sggp.org.vn/ho-chi-minh-city-brings-ai-education-into-classrooms-post130954.html)). The bottlenecks named included teacher capacity, funding, student data safety and formalistic assessment. Vietnam's largest city is working the same problems as the international schools around it, on a national timetable.

**Gemini in Gmail reached every adult Workspace account, on by default.** From 10 November, Google is extending thread summaries and non-personalised Help me write to "all Workspace users 18 years and older" ([Google, 10 November](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/11/expanding-gemini-in-gmail-to-more-Google-Workspace-users.html)). The features are on by default; users can disable them and admins can switch them off. The post does not name Education editions. For a school, that means every staff inbox and any student account set as adult. The decision is better made before the habit forms.

## Sceptic's Corner

The protest slogan wants Search without the AI. The strongest case against it is that the old results page was never the gateway campaigners remember.

In clickstream data that mostly predates AI Overviews, SparkToro and Datos found that "58.5% of American Google searches resulted in zero clicks", and that for every 1,000 US searches just 360 clicks reached a site Google doesn't own ([SparkToro, 2 July 2024](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study-for-every-1000-us-google-searches-only-374-clicks-go-to-the-open-web-in-the-eu-its-360/)). Answer boxes, panels and the habit of stopping at the top all came before the chatbot. The Overview made an old pattern fluent.

That doesn't make the Overview harmless. A box that writes the homework on request is a fair thing to want gone for younger students, and Common Sense's evidence gives campaigners a real case. But switching it off would not restore a habit of checking sources that most users never had. Worse, the fight over the switch lets everyone treat the problem as solved once the switch is found.

The Stanford trial is the better guide. Six lessons doubled students' ability to spot a dubious site and still left them on half marks. That is the work, with or without the box.

The line for the staffroom: the AI box isn't the first time Google answered for students; it's the first time it answered in paragraphs. Teach them to leave the page.
