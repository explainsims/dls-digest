## Top story

**Hanoi requires teachers to demonstrate readiness before teaching core AI lessons.** The city's education department issued a directive on 23 November requiring Level 2 preparation for every teacher assigned to deliver the 12 core periods of AI education in a secondary class this school year, including teachers scheduled for the second semester. Hanoi's earlier city training, held from 5 to 15 November, had 2,071 teachers nominated from 664 institutions. Of those teachers, 664 taught subjects other than computing, chiefly mathematics, physics, literature, English and chemistry. The department counted 54 secondary schools that had sent no computing teacher and told those schools to review their arrangements. [Vietnam Government Electronic Newspaper, 23 November](https://thanglong.chinhphu.vn/ha-noi-yeu-cau-giao-vien-hoan-thanh-boi-duong-muc-2-truoc-khi-day-ai-103261123210135713.htm).

Teachers who missed the city training can qualify through at least 24 periods of school-based preparation across subject-team sessions, guided independent study and joint lesson review. To establish equivalence, they need an approved 12-period teaching plan for a grade and a demonstration or practice lesson rated satisfactory by their subject team. Schools must plan an activity that can run without devices and check AI tools before use. The school-based training guidance bars real student faces, voices and personal information from training outputs, learning materials and demonstration lessons. It suggests photographs of objects, drawings, hand gestures or coded cards as substitutes. Lower-secondary pupils should not have to register personal accounts. Schools are due to file their plans and teacher lists by 30 November, ahead of teaching. [Vietnam Government Electronic Newspaper, 23 November](https://thanglong.chinhphu.vn/ha-noi-yeu-cau-giao-vien-hoan-thanh-boi-duong-muc-2-truoc-khi-day-ai-103261123210135713.htm).

The demonstration is the part Ed Tech Coaches can borrow. Give a physics teacher a set of drawings for a classifier and ask the subject team to watch what happens when it sees a drawing they held back. Can students explain why the model missed it, and can the teacher keep the lesson going if the site is down? Those two questions get closer to readiness than a record of who attended the training.

## Other stories

- **Google makes managed access to Workspace generally available for AI agents.** On 24 November Google announced general availability for its Workspace Model Context Protocol servers and its official command-line tool. The servers give connected agents a managed way to act across services such as Gmail, Drive, Calendar and Docs. Google says administrators will be able to govern agent access, require user confirmation for sensitive actions, and review logs of agent activity and confirmations. It says existing user-level data-loss prevention rules apply. The tools and controls are rolling out over two weeks, with technical enforcement of the new Workspace access rules for AI agents starting on 1 February 2027. For a Workspace school, the immediate task is to locate agents that can already read, change or share school data. A useful test uses a synthetic file and a test account. Ask an approved agent to prepare a draft and then to share the file outside the domain. Inspect what happens at each step, including what the audit log records. [Google Workspace Updates, 24 November](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/11/mcp-servers-cli-standardized-usage-tiers-now-available.html).

- **A US bill puts the learning claim and the data contract on the same page.** Representative Becca Balint introduced the Education Technology Standards Act on 24 November. The proposal directs the National Institute of Standards and Technology and the Department of Education to develop guidance and model contracts for K–12 technology. Topics in the text include user-data collection and deletion, evidence of educational benefit, and a way for teachers to report factual errors and for suppliers to fix them. It would require reasonable care to protect identifiable student data and authorise $3 million in fiscal year 2027 for university-led evaluations. The What Works Clearinghouse would review the studies and publish effectiveness ratings based on those that meet its standards. The error clause is worth borrowing now. A teacher should have a route to report a wrong answer in a product, get an acknowledgement and learn what changed. If a faulty explanation stays live for the other classes until the next release, the school has learned something about the supplier's support and about who carries the review work. [Balint announcement, 24 November](https://balint.house.gov/news/documentsingle.aspx?DocumentID=871) · [Bill text](https://balint.house.gov/uploadedfiles/education_technology_standards_act_full_text.pdf).

- **TikTok redesigns Family Pairing and adds a guide for educators.** In its Singapore newsroom on 23 November, TikTok described a dashboard that puts screen time up front and lets linked parents see changes in follower and following counts, a sample of material from verified creators their teen follows, and the week's public posts. The redesign is also available through a browser. TikTok listed visibility of whom teens message as forthcoming. It also launched an educator guide with more classroom resources promised. That makes two things to distinguish in a family briefing. There are the switches a parent can change and the information the platform chooses to display. A sample from verified accounts is one small view of a feed. A counselor can use it to open a conversation about what keeps showing up, how a recommendation got there and what the student does when the answer is unwelcome. For teachers, the guide can explain how particular features work. Claims about their effects still need evidence from outside the platform. [TikTok Singapore newsroom, 23 November](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/tiktok-launches-redesigned-family-pairing-to-support-parents-with-actionable-insights-sg?lang=en-SG).

- **Gemini Notebook puts pictures and a stopwatch on flashcards.** Google posted the Workspace release on 24 November, listing images drawn from uploaded sources, an optional stopwatch and review reminders in the mobile app. Google lists the features as available to all Workspace customers and gives administrators no separate switch for this update. Images are the one change here that could alter the kind of question a teacher asks. A circuit diagram can become a prompt to predict which bulb goes out if a switch opens. The same diagram can give the whole thing away if Google imports its labels or a worked solution. Before sharing a deck, check the face students see against the answer you actually want them to reconstruct. A reminder can help schedule another try, and a timer can be useful for facts where fluent recall matters. For a longer explanation, let the clock sit idle and ask for the reason the student chose that answer. [Google Workspace Updates, 24 November](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/11/new-study-enhancements-for-flashcards-in-Gemini-Notebook.html).

## On the radar

- **Iowa opens its draft computer science, data science and AI standards for comment.** The state education department opened consultation on 23 November. It is seeking feedback on revised computing standards and new standards for data science and AI, with hybrid forums on 8, 14 and 16 December and a survey closing on 23 December. The review team's recommendations go to the state board. The eventual text is worth comparing with Hanoi's implementation. ## Sceptic's corner

Google gives the stopwatch in Gemini Notebook a job simulating timed exam conditions. It is a plausible job for an exam that already expects facts or procedures to be available quickly. The leap is treating a shorter time through a familiar deck as a sign that a student is ready for an exam with unfamiliar questions. A learner might get quicker at recognising the particular diagrams the deck shows. When the same underlying system is drawn a different way, the cues they memorised can disappear. The 24 November update describes the tools. It supplies no learning-outcome comparison. [Google Workspace Updates, 24 November](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/11/new-study-enhancements-for-flashcards-in-Gemini-Notebook.html).

Start with a pupil trying to explain why opening a switch stops current in one branch while the other keeps working. First let them recall a familiar circuit. The next day, give them a rearranged drawing, take away the labels and ask them to mark the path and explain their reasoning. Both runs produce usable evidence. The first can tell a teacher whether retrieval has become easier. The second can show whether the learner has a model that survives a new drawing. If there is room on the lesson plan for one measure of progress, keep the new drawing. The stopwatch can stay with the flashcards.
