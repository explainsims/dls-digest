# Week 19, week ending Sunday, 10 May 2026

## The thread of the week

In five working days the LMS conversation stopped being about features and started being about governance posture, and the field moved with it.

Monday opened with Harvard FAS [dropping ChatGPT Edu in favour of a Claude-plus-Gemini portfolio](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2026/4/28/fas-anthropic-claude/) — a Harvard-tier institution walking back a single-vendor commitment exactly as 2026-27 LMS RFP cycles are firming up at international schools. Wednesday filed [the Canvas multi-vendor architecture](https://www.instructure.com/press-release/instructure-introduces-simplified-canvas-tiers-and-ecosystem-updates-new-next) as the working reference: three named external AI providers ([OpenAI in-assignment](https://www.instructure.com/press-release/instructure-and-openai-announce-global-partnership-embed-ai-learning-experiences), [Anthropic Claude over LTI for student-facing tutoring](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11725453-set-up-the-claude-lti-in-canvas-by-instructure), [Anthropic Claude on Bedrock for the IgniteAI admin agent](https://www.constellationr.com/insights/news/instructure-launches-igniteai-agent-canvas-builds-amazon-bedrock)), plumbed through three open-standards seams (LTI 1.3, MCP, bring-your-own-key) — the first big LMS that's genuinely structured itself for vendor swap. The same Wednesday filed the [Toddle](https://www.toddleapp.com/) counterweight: IB-native, Bengaluru-headquartered, dual-cloud-hidden AI architecture (Bedrock-Claude and Azure-OpenAI inside Toddle's own perimeter, [PII never leaving the trust boundary](https://www.toddleapp.com/3rdparty/)), no LTI-equivalent for swapping the AI surface — the *opposite* bet on swappability for *stronger* data isolation by construction. Thursday [the Canvas breach reframed the question](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/05/hackers-steal-students-data-during-breach-at-education-tech-giant-instructure/). Friday [the breach became the answer](https://databreaches.net/2026/05/07/developing-shinyhunters-hacks-instructure-again-canvas-down/). Three Instructure breaches in eight months — the third *after* the public crisis-response patches landed — and the procurement question is no longer "which AI tutor" but "whose remediation cycle do you trust to run alongside your gradebook for the next four years." Friday's Instructure analysis added the Bain Capital ruling: a [19 March SDCal decision keeping Bain in *In re PowerSchool*](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/insights/alerts/unprecedented-private-equity-firm-potentially-hook-portfolio-companys-data-breach) on a "ratified and conditioned" cost-cuts theory — the disclaimer-of-control template that protects PE-owned vendors from portfolio-company breach liability now has a survivable plaintiff theory pointed at it. Friday's framework scoping piece closed the arc: students are going somewhere with AI, teachers are going somewhere else, [RAND has the 1.8x usage gap and the 80%-of-students-not-taught number](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA4180-1.html), and a school that wants to *curate* the trajectory rather than catch up to it needs a digital-fluency framework underneath whatever AI policy it ships — chassis first, AI on top.

That's the through-line. Five days, four pieces, one shape: **AI-in-LMS is now a procurement question about governance posture, and the curriculum question underneath it is whose digital-fluency chassis you build on.**

## Emerging patterns

**Vendor breach posture is becoming a first-class procurement variable, and existing rubrics aren't ready for it.** Most LMS RFP rubrics treat security as yes/no checkboxes — does the vendor have an InfoSec function, hold SOC 2, disclose breaches in the last 24 months. That shape produces the same answer for one contained incident and three incidents in eight months including one post-remediation. The 2026-27 cycle needs numerically-weighted, evidence-based questions about the *remediation cycle*: number of public incidents within an 8-month window, most-recent pen-test summary date and high/critical findings, vulnerability-disclosure programme depth (public bug bounty vs portal-request-only), incident-response timelines in hours not adverbs ("notify within 72 hours" scores; "without undue delay" doesn't). The Bain ruling adds a sixth question for PE-owned vendors specifically — documented evidence of cybersecurity-staffing levels relative to pre-acquisition baseline, open-press evidence accepted, vendor self-attestation noted but not weighted. Rubric authors weren't writing this question in 2024. They are now.

**The AI-inside-suite layer is converging across every major vendor, and the differentiation race has moved.** BETT week landed [Google's free Gemini 3 Pro for educators](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/bett-2026-gemini-classroom-updates/), [Microsoft's Teach module shipping globally inside the M365 Copilot app](https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/microsoft-launches-new-teach-ai-tool-in-copilot-app-to-mixed-reviews-from-educators) (lesson plans / rubrics / flashcards / quizzes mapped to 35+ countries' standards), [Anthropic's MS365 deep-integration](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-06/anthropic-inks-computing-deal-with-spacex-to-meet-ai-demand) (Word / PowerPoint / Excel / Outlook), and [Toddle's five AI-powered learning interfaces](https://www.toddleapp.com/toddle-demo-day-february-2026/) inside the LMS — all within ten working days of each other. The temptation is to read this as a vendor-arms-race. The honest read is the inverse: every major suite now has roughly the same surface-area assistant baked in, which means the procurement decision schools are making in 2026-27 isn't *which assistant* but *which assistant sits closest to the gradebook and what its data-flow disclosure looks like at that surface*. That's an LMS-architecture question. Which is why the breach week and the BETT week are the same story.

**The "students-going-sideways" gap is now data, not anecdote.** [RAND's 2025 panel](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA4180-1.html): teachers are 1.8x more likely than students to use ChatGPT weekly, but students lead in tutoring (64%) and college/career advice (49%); 80%+ of US students said teachers had not taught them how to use AI for schoolwork. That isn't a generational head-start — it's a curatorial gap. Schools that lean on "they're digital natives" framing in 2026 are repeating a 2005 mistake — the [literature has been clear for over a decade](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10123718/) that exposure ≠ literacy. The pathway-curation question — *who is shaping the trajectory students are already on* — is the load-bearing one for any digital-fluency framework adoption.

**Single-vendor AI campus deals are visibly walking back.** Harvard FAS dropping ChatGPT Edu (low uptake plus cost). [Cal State's $17M ChatGPT Edu rollout in active student/faculty pushback](https://laist.com/news/education/cal-state-struck-a-deal-with-openai-some-students-and-faculty-refuse-to-use-it) (52% of faculty report negative effect on teaching; 67% of students feel professors don't teach them how to use AI effectively). [Anthropic adding full-campus deals at Northeastern, LSE, and Champlain](https://www.anthropic.com/news/advancing-claude-for-education) — but as portfolio additions, not exclusive lock-ins. The pattern is *portfolio over partnership* — institutions are building capacity to swap, not commit, and the field is reorganising around that posture. The implication is mundane and correct: vendors will keep leapfrogging each other through 2026-27, the question is not "who wins" but "what's the process for swapping the underperformer out without disrupting teaching."

**Personalised-phishing is the second-order story from the Canvas breach, and it's a 90-day window.** The leak's load-bearing content isn't names and student IDs — [it's the inboxes and discussion-thread messages](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/personalised-phishing-attacks-likely-after-global-canvas-hack) between students and teachers. With real voice, real context, and real conversation history, the next wave of phishing won't be generic; it'll reference real courses and real conversations, raising helpdesk-impersonation success rates against schools that have nothing to do with Canvas directly. Stolen-data phishing typically peaks 30-90 days post-leak and tails for 6-12 months. That puts the personalised-phishing window across northern-hemisphere term opening through mid-year — which is when most international schools are absorbing transition fatigue from the next academic year. IT teams should run the MFA enrolment audit and brief staff on the specific attack pattern *now*, while it's fresh.

## Sceptic's corner

**The "Canvas is finished" read overshoots.** Three breaches in eight months, with the third after public remediation, is a structural failure of the remediation cycle and a reasonable reason to weight Instructure down in active RFPs. It is not a reason to expect 8,809 institutions to migrate inside an 18-month cycle. The institutional inertia is enormous — a decade of course content, gradebook history, SSO integration, faculty workflow muscle-memory. Most current customers will stay. Renewals will get harder. RFPs will get won less often. Price will give. *That's* the real shape of this story over the next 18 months, and a school treating "Canvas is dead" as the operating premise is reading a procurement-pressure story as a market-collapse story. The [Higher Ed Intelligence framing](https://higheredintel.substack.com/p/the-instructure-breach-exposed-more) was right: the operational effect of repeated vendor breaches is the gradual lowering of the threshold for competitive evaluation during future buying cycles, not immediate customer loss.

**The "Toddle is the safe pick because no public breaches" read also overshoots.** Absence of disclosed breaches is not evidence of breach-resistance — it can mean strong posture, smaller attack surface, or just not having been targeted yet (Toddle has 2,500+ schools, Canvas has ~9,000+; the attack-surface math isn't symmetrical). The [Common Sense Media review](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/ai-ratings/toddle) applauded Toddle's encryption-and-anonymisation but flagged that AI-literacy scaffolding shipped with the product is thin and that AI-progress-reports drawing inferences from incomplete information is a real risk to student wellbeing. The [AISB student paper](https://thebite.aisb.ro/switching-to-toddle-a-change-that-missed-the-mark/) documented over 75% parent dissatisfaction in one peer-school transition — UX-complexity and notification-volume tax is the most-cited negative in real-school user feedback. The procurement-honest framing is: Toddle and Canvas are two different bets on what to optimise for (data isolation vs swappability), and the Sceptic's question for either is "what's your plan for the day this vendor has its incident."

**The "AI literacy frameworks are the answer" read needs the Williamson/Watters/Narayanan-Kapoor caveat.** Once the [OECD/EC AILit framework](https://ailiteracyframework.org/) becomes the foundation of PISA 2029's Media and AI Literacy assessment, schools will teach to that test four years in advance — that's [the Williamson critique](https://codeactsineducation.wordpress.com/2025/04/30/performing-ai-literacy/), and it's the load-bearing objection in the field. A framework whose AI competencies are *only* skill-acquisition (prompt, evaluate, create) and not also *critical orientation* (whose interests does this AI serve, when does it function as snake oil, when should I refuse to use it) is going to age badly. The four questions to ask any framework — does it distinguish exposure from fluency, does it treat AI as a layer or as a parallel, does it handle students-going-sideways, does it price in critical orientation — are the right gate before adoption. Most candidate frameworks pass two of the four. None pass all four cleanly.

**The Bain Capital ruling is not yet a precedent, it's a plaintiff-theory survival.** Bain stayed in *In re PowerSchool* at the motion-to-dismiss stage, which is a plausibility standard, not a merits standard. PE-firm liability in portfolio-company breach cases is *new legal weather*, not *settled law*. Any procurement decision built on "PE-owned vendors are now legally exposed" is overreaching the case. The honest read: PE-owned vendors are now in a different legal-risk posture than 18 months ago, magnitude TBD over the next two years of litigation. Enough to change rubric weighting. Not enough to disqualify by ownership type alone.
