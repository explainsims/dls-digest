# 3 May 2026

## Top story — Boston Public Schools to make AI fluency a graduation requirement

**Boston Public Schools is becoming the first major-city US district to require AI literacy for graduation**, with mandatory programming launching across all BPS high schools in September 2026. That's three months before international schools open their 2026-27 school year — and it puts a stake in the ground that schools won't be able to ignore for long. ([source](https://www.thethinkacademy.com/blog/ai-in-the-classroom-whats-actually-happening-in-u-s-schools-in-2026/))

**Why this matters for international schools:** Ed Tech Coaches are the natural home for the question *"do we need an AI fluency requirement, and what would it look like?"* BPS is moving first, which means a working example to study rather than reverse-engineer. International schools tend to follow US curricular trends with a lag, and the IB has been signalling movement on AI-and-critical-thinking work for over a year. Worth tracking the BPS framework as it unfolds rather than reacting late.

## Other stories

- **NYC DOE has opened public feedback on AI guidance** for its 1.1m-student system. Teachers permitted for brainstorming, organising, and lesson planning; AI cannot be used for grading, disciplinary decisions, or biometric/behavioural data without strict oversight. Feedback window closes **8 May 2026** — fast turnaround, but useful as a school-AI-policy template. ([MultiState legislative roundup](https://www.multistate.us/insider/2026/4/9/how-states-are-regulating-ai-in-education-this-legislative-session))
- **Major model cycle since late April.** OpenAI shipped GPT-5.5 and GPT-5.5 Pro for parallel reasoning. Anthropic released Claude Opus 4.6 and Sonnet 4.6, with Opus 4.7 already current; the 1M-token context window is now standard with no surcharge — whole textbooks in a single prompt. Google released Gemini 3.1 Ultra (2M context) and Flash-Lite. ([LLM Stats roundup](https://llm-stats.com/llm-updates))
- **Google I/O 2026 lands 19 May.** Previewed: Gemini 4, AI glasses with Warby Parker, and a robotics partnership putting Gemini inside Boston Dynamics' Atlas. Calendar-worthy for any Google school. ([Mejba's preview](https://www.mejba.me/blog/google-io-2026-ai-announcements))
- **Teacher AI adoption hit 61% in 2025**, up from 34% in 2023. 69% report it improved their teaching methods; 55% say it gave them more time to interact with students. The reverse stat: under half have received any institutional training — exactly the gap Ed Tech Coaches exist to close. ([programs.com stats](https://programs.com/resources/ai-education-statistics/))
- **134 AI-in-education bills** introduced across 31 US states in 2026 — data privacy, classroom restrictions, curriculum integration. International schools aren't governed by these, but the policy templates and parent expectations will travel.

## On the radar

- **Generative → agentic shift.** AI labs are repositioning from content-generators to autonomous task-executors this quarter. Dominating vendor messaging. Wait for school-relevant examples before treating as more than positioning.
- **Rasmussen University moving from Blackboard to D2L Brightspace** with AI-native study recommendations baked in. Vendor messaging here is a useful signal of where the LMS market is heading.
- **OECD Digital Education Outlook 2026 is live.** Mostly framework-level, but a useful artefact for presenting AI-in-education thinking to administrators who want institutional cover. ([OECD report](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-digital-education-outlook-2026_062a7394-en.html))

## Sceptic's corner

Underneath the adoption headline there's a quieter set of numbers worth holding alongside it: **70% of teachers worry AI weakens critical thinking and research skills**, **72% worry about academic integrity**, and **over half of students report feeling less connected to their teachers** when AI is used in class (Center for Democracy and Technology, late 2025). The Ed Tech Coaches conversation isn't just about getting more AI into classrooms — it's about getting it in *without* the connection-and-cognition cost that's already showing up in teacher and student survey data.
