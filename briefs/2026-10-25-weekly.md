## What Does It All Mean?

Last week's answer was that manner matters more than dose. Maryland raises the next question: who sets the manner? Is an AI tutor something a school switches on, or something a teacher designs?

Read past the headline, a fall of up to 0.37 standard deviations in final grades, and the Maryland paper half-answers it ([IZA](https://docs.iza.org/dp18989.pdf)). The tutor had a mode that guides students toward an answer instead of supplying one. The default was direct instruction, and only two instructors ended the term with tutor mode on. About 15% of students offered the tool used it even once. Of their requests, 73.8% sought information, explanations or solutions; 0.7% sought feedback on their own work. The authors allow that the tool's arrival may have shifted perceived permission to use AI generally, so the result may measure a signal as much as a product.

The trials where AI tutoring worked look different. At Harvard, Greg Kestin and colleagues built their physics tutor's prompts around active engagement, cognitive load and growth mindset, and walked students through each problem in sequence. Students learned more, in less time, than in an active-learning class ([Scientific Reports, June 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12179260/)). A six-week after-school programme in Nigeria produced gains of about 0.3 standard deviations; the World Bank team's verdict is that the tool works as a tutor when implemented "with teacher support" ([World Bank, January 2025](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/education/From-chalkboards-to-chatbots-Transforming-learning-in-Nigeria)). Stanford's Tutor CoPilot put the AI behind the human: students whose tutors had it were 4 percentage points more likely to master topics, and 9 points with lower-rated tutors ([Wang and colleagues, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03017)).

The cleanest high-school test isolates the variable. Hamsa Bastani's team gave nearly 1,000 maths students in Turkey either a plain ChatGPT-style interface or a tutor that "includes teacher input and is designed to guide students with hints rather than directly giving answers" ([Wharton, August 2024](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education)). On the unaided exam, the plain group scored 17% below students who never had AI. The tutor group avoided that loss ([PNAS, July 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nas/journl/v122y2025pe2422633122.html)).

So the working answer: AI tutoring helps when someone has designed what it does, when, and what follows. Made merely available, it becomes an answer machine, and the defaults decide the rest.

That matters for schools running Gemini, because Google's defaults are generous. The Gemini student hub, with study notebooks, flashcards and quizzes, reaches Education users of all ages, governed only by the admin console's generative AI settings ([Google, 1 October](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/find-your-learning-tools-all-in-one-place-with-the-student-hub-in-Gemini.html)). That is Maryland's condition at scale. A teacher-assigned Gemini Notebook is nearer Harvard's. The number worth tracking this year is not accounts provisioned but tasks designed, each followed by a short unaided check.

## Behind You! Behind You!

**Only one US state requires AI tools to be checked for their effect on learning.** CRPE's annual State of the American Student report counts 36 states with published or endorsed AI guidance, most of it advisory ([CRPE](https://stateofthestudent.crpe.org/)). The only one requiring AI tools to be vetted for academic outcomes is Maryland ([The 74, 21 October](https://www.the74million.org/article/only-1-in-10-students-attends-a-district-that-has-bounced-back-from-the-pandemic/)), home of the university behind this week's sobering tutor trial. International schools have no state doing even that; for them, the only vetting layer is the school itself.

**Anthropic handed the Agent Skills standard to a neutral foundation.** On [22 October](https://www.anthropic.com/news/donating-agent-skills-to-the-agentic-ai-foundation) it donated the format to the Agentic AI Foundation, the Linux Foundation body it co-founded with Block and OpenAI. Anthropic says more than 70 agent platforms, including ChatGPT and GitHub Copilot, have integrated it. Google's own transition page says Gemini skills use "the open, Markdown-based `SKILL.md` format" ([Google](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/generative-ai/gemini-app/about-the-transition-to-skills)). So a teacher's skill built in Gemini should travel between tools. "Should" is doing work there; test one before promising anyone.

**Google Vault can now export Gemini conversations.** Under the [22 October update](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/google-vault-now-supports-filtered-exports-for-Gemini-in-Workspace.html), admins on Education Fundamentals, Standard and Plus can filter exports by user or organisational unit, up to 5,000 users, and by date range. It covers Gemini in Gmail, Drive, Docs, Sheets, Slides and Meet when conversation history is on; retention rules and litigation holds come later. Gemini chats inside Docs and Gmail are now retrievable records. Who may request them, and why, deserves a written answer before the first request.

**Meet's speech translation now covers 63 languages.** The [22 October update](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/real-time-speech-translation-in-google-Meet-expands-to-63-languages-with-automatic-language-detection.html) allows any combination of them, with automatic language detection. In schools it needs the Google AI Pro for Education add-on; Scheduled Release domains start on 2 November, and base plans cap sessions at 90 minutes. Google doesn't list the 63, so check the languages a school's families actually speak before anyone promises a parent a translated conference.

## Sceptic's Corner

Next time someone says the answer is simply a well-designed tutor, offer three facts.

First, design reliably prevented harm; it did not reliably produce learning. Bastani's guard-railed tutor lifted practice scores by 127%, yet its students "scored about the same on the exam as the control group", who never had AI ([Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education)).

Second, much of the positive evidence comes from builders marking their own homework. The Harvard team built the tutor it tested, and the paper notes that its authors overlap with those behind the active-learning method it beat ([Scientific Reports](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12179260/)). A September preprint on GCSE science found a solid effect (g = 0.33) for the Medly platform against self-directed revision; Medly funded the evaluation, and attrition was 30.7% ([arXiv, September 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14789)).

Third, the horizons are short: two weeks at Harvard, four in England, six in Nigeria. Nobody yet knows what a year of well-designed AI does to a fifteen-year-old's ability to work without it.

So the defensible claim is narrower than the pitch. Designed AI use can avoid the damage open access does. Whether it beats a good teacher given the same minutes has been shown, so far, mainly in one Harvard physics course. Guardrails are a seatbelt, not an engine.
