5 May 2026

Top story — Harvard FAS drops ChatGPT Edu for Claude, and the single-vendor era of campus AI is quietly over

Harvard’s Faculty of Arts and Sciences confirmed last week it is phasing out ChatGPT Edu after June 2026 and adding Anthropic’s Claude Code to its supported toolkit. Gemini remains the primary FAS service for students; Claude is added alongside; ChatGPT becomes opt-in with budgetary approval. Reasons cited: low student uptake and cost. Anthropic the same week announced full-campus Claude agreements with Northeastern, LSE, and Champlain, plus Canvas LTI integration, MCP servers for Wiley and Panopto, and a “Learning mode” that scaffolds reasoning instead of answering. [Harvard Crimson] · [Anthropic]

Why it matters at SSIS: the framing has shifted from “which vendor do we standardise on” to “which portfolio do we offer and how do we evaluate them.” That’s the right question heading into the 2026-27 LMS transition — the worst time to lock in a single AI provider via a new LMS contract is exactly now, when Harvard-tier institutions are actively walking back single-vendor commitments.

The school is a Google school, so Gemini is the practical default — but a portfolio-friendly stance is the right SSIS Digital Learning Specialist posture: Claude available where it’s better (long-context document work, coding, scaffolded reasoning), Gemini where workflows live in Workspace, and an explicit policy that no single vendor gets a curriculum-deep integration without a review cycle.

Sceptic’s corner

The “Claude beats ChatGPT” narrative is louder than the data.

A wave of April commentary — including some respectable analysis — frames Harvard’s decision as part of a broader Claude-overtaking-ChatGPT story, citing revenue figures and a Pentagon contract dispute. Take this with appropriate salt. Harvard’s actual reasons in the Crimson reporting are mundane: low student uptake of ChatGPT Edu specifically, and cost. Gemini remains the FAS default. That’s a procurement-and-portfolio story, not a vendor-supremacy story.

The implication for SSIS is the boring-but-correct one: vendors will keep leapfrogging each other through 2026-27. The question is not “who wins” but “what’s our process for swapping the underperformer out without disrupting teaching.” Build for swap, not for loyalty.
