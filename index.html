8 May 2026

Top story — Instructure was breached again on 7 May, Canvas went dark across major institutions, and the deadline shifted to 12 May. What Wednesday’s coverage called a procurement signal is now a procurement event.

Earlier coverage framed today as the leak deadline. It isn’t, any more — but only because ShinyHunters escalated rather than leaked. On Thursday afternoon US-time, Canvas began intermittently failing across higher-ed; UC Irvine students saw a pop-up at 13:05 local; UC Berkeley, Princeton, MIT, Penn, Duke, Harvard, Baylor, Rutgers and a long list of K-12 districts (Charlotte-Mecklenburg, Wake County, the NC schools group) followed. Several customer login pages were defaced with injected HTML — TechCrunch confirmed three; the actual count is higher in the school-press coverage. The defacement message names the new deadline: “You have till the end of the day by 12 May 2026 before everything is leaked.” Instructure took Canvas infrastructure offline for “scheduled maintenance,” contained the intrusion, revoked privileged credentials, and as of filing time has not commented on the extortion. ShinyHunters’ framing this round is sharper than last week’s: “Instead of contacting us to resolve it they ignored us and did some ‘security patches’.” [DataBreaches.net] · [TechCrunch] · [Inside Higher Ed] · [WRAL] · [Daily Pennsylvanian] · [UC Irvine New University] · [The Tech (MIT)] · [Baylor ITS] · [Daily Princetonian]

The structural read is clean. Instructure has now been breached three times in eight months — the disclosed October 2025 incident, the 3 May ransom claim, and the 7 May re-entry/defacement. The third one happened after the public crisis-response patches, which is the worst possible signal a vendor can send mid-contract-cycle. The boundary between “an unfortunate breach” and “a vendor-risk profile” closed yesterday afternoon. Higher-ed analyst chatter is converging on personalised-phishing risk as the second-order story: with names, school emails, student IDs and teacher-student message contents, attackers can reproduce voice and context well enough to ride the next attack into single-sign-on harvesting. [Times Higher Education]

The procurement signal for international schools is straightforward. Wednesday’s framing was “vendor breach posture as legitimate rubric input.” Today’s is “three breaches in eight months, with the third coming after public remediation.” If LMS evaluation rubrics still treat security as a checkbox (“vendor maintains an InfoSec program: yes/no”), this is the week to ask whether that’s adequate against a 12 May leak likely to include teacher-student messages from peer institutions. The 12 May deadline falls on a Tuesday.

Sceptic’s corner

Two corrections to earlier framing — and one to the wider narrative.

First: Wednesday’s incident was called Instructure’s second breach in eight months. With yesterday’s re-entry, it’s the third, and the third happened after the patches went in. That changes the rhetorical weight of the procurement argument from “this vendor has a problem” to “this vendor’s remediation cycle did not work” — a different and harder thing for Instructure to recover from in the next earnings cycle.

Second: ShinyHunters’ “275 million users” claim still hasn’t been independently verified, and the 8,809-institution victim list has had publicly-disputed entries (some named institutions told local press they had no Canvas tenancy at all). Treat scale claims as ceiling, not floor; the practical risk to any individual student is determined by what the leak sample actually contains, which is still unknown.

Third — to the wider narrative: BETT week always produces a vendor-arms-race read. Google’s free Gemini 3 Pro for educators, Microsoft’s Teach module, Anthropic’s M365 deep-integration and Toddle’s AI Tutor Awards all landed within ten days of each other. The temptation is to read this as “the AI-in-LMS war is on” and rush into a stack decision. The honest read is the inverse: every major vendor now has roughly the same surface-area assistant baked into their own suite. The decision schools are actually making in 2026-27 is whose surface area sits closest to the gradebook, and that’s a procurement question, not an AI-feature question. The Canvas breach week happens to be highlighting exactly that — what’s at stake when the AI-inside-LMS gets the wrong vendor.

6 May 2026 · Toddle — IB-native LMS, dual-cloud AI

Lede Toddle’s AI plumbing is more private-by-construction than Canvas (PII never leaves Toddle’s trust boundary; AI Insights fully self-hosted) and less swappable (single vendor, single product, no LTI agent layer). Whether that’s the right trade for an international school is exactly the question. What it is IB-native LMS HQ’d in Bengaluru (founded 2019); 2,500+ schools across 100+ countries; FOBISIA affiliate. Anthropic Claude via AWS Bedrock + OpenAI via Azure (both inside Toddle’s own cloud accounts) + self-hosted LLMs for AI Insights. Five Demo Days in 15 months; AI grading, AI Insights, five student-facing AI surfaces, NAPLAN/PAT/MAP ingestion just shipped. What schools could borrow A by-construction data-isolation architecture is a coherent alternative to standards-based swappability — either bet is defensible. Compliance stack is competitive (SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, ISO 27701, 1EdTech TrustEd Apps, iKeepSafe FERPA+COPPA). Multi-region data residency including a politically-isolated China region matters for international schools with multi-jurisdiction parent data. Ten RFP questions worth putting on the table directly — including bias-audit methodology, AI-artefact portability on contract end, and Series B / runway disclosure. Sceptic AISB Bucharest’s student paper documented a not-clean migration off Google Classroom + ManageBac — UX-complexity tax + notification-volume the recurring complaints. Common Sense Media gave “Pass Full” on privacy but flagged AI-literacy scaffolding shipped with the product as thin. AI grading gets the most independent challenge. Funding profile ($29.6M total raised; $6M January 2025 round looks bridge-shaped, no Series B announced) is worth asking about directly.

6 May 2026 · Canvas multi-vendor architecture

Lede Canvas's three-AI-provider architecture is the first working multi-vendor LMS reference — and Instructure's second breach in eight months landed the same week. What it is OpenAI for in-assignment LLM activities (Gradebook-integrated, BYO-key); AWS Bedrock + Anthropic Claude for IgniteAI Agent (educator/admin agentic tasks, MCP-extensible); Anthropic Claude via LTI for the student-facing tutor surface — inside a new Core / Plus / Next tier structure with free-AI cliffs at 30 June and 30 September 2026.
